A suddenly loaded crack in a fluid-saturated solid: can AI agents find the drained tip?

Read the mechanics: model and solutionSee how the agents did

Detournay and Cheng (1991) loaded a stationary crack in a Biot solid by a sudden face traction or a sudden face pore pressure. We gave their problem, without the paper, to AI agents. Most answers recovered the crack-scale elastic limits. The decisive step was the crack tip, where the solid drains at once even though the crack as a whole responds undrained.

Aim

To see whether agents select the right physical limit at a singular point, carry it through to every requested quantity, and check what they report against the problem they were given.

Our question
Given only the governing equations and the two loadings, can an AI agent find the stress intensities and crack volumes at short and long times, including the tip intensity just after loading and the leading growth of the volume?
Posed unchanged to every run. The paper, its answers and the reference were withheld.
The answer and its present limit

Every run in the comparison found the crack-scale limits: the undrained and drained volumes and the long-time intensity. The difference was at the tip. GPT, in three of three runs, and Opus 5.5, in its single run, treated the tip as drained inside an undrained outer field and obtained K₁(0⁺) = 0.8076√(πL). Every other run took the crack-scale value √(πL) at the tip, and with it missed a tip term in the volume growth as large as the face term.

Loading 2 separated the runs a second time. Its long-time state is a pressurized region still confined by the solid around it, which closes the crack with a back-stress −η. Most other answers treated the region as free to swell (zero) or replaced it by an effective face traction (−α).

What the agents did · what separated them · why it matters

What the agents did

All runs recovered the undrained start and the drained end at the crack scale. Four runs, three by GPT and one by Opus, found the drained-tip value (MP-39 called its own value provisional); the others reasoned with elasticity at one modulus at a time.

What separated them

Two limits, not one. The short-time limit ct/L² → 0 makes the crack scale undrained, but the tip is drained at every positive time because its own drainage time r²/c is zero. Only the runs that kept r/√(ct) as a second variable found the tip value and the tip volume term.

Why it matters

A correct global argument applied at the wrong scale gave confident, internally checked answers. The checks reproduced the assumption and could not catch it.

1 · The model

A crack in a fluid-saturated solid

A stationary crack of length 2L lies in an infinite, isotropic Biot solid in plane strain. The pores are filled with fluid that flows by Darcy’s law. Stress and pore pressure couple in both directions: compressing the solid raises the pore pressure, and pore pressure strains the skeleton.

Two time scales matter. If the load changes faster than fluid can move, the fluid content of each material point is fixed and the solid behaves elastically with the undrained Poisson ratio νu. Once the pore pressure has diffused away it behaves elastically with the drained ratio ν. Diffusion over a distance ℓ takes a time ℓ²/c, so after a time t the drained region extends about √(ct) from any drained boundary.

Two loadings act on both crack faces from t = 0. In loading 1 the faces carry a unit pressure and are kept drained, p = 0. In loading 2 the faces carry no traction but a unit pore pressure. The task asked for K and the crack volume V at t = 0⁺ and as t → ∞, the leading growth of V at small times, and the pore pressure induced next to the faces, for ν = 0.2, νu = 0.4 and α = 0.89.

As in the paper Model, loadings and material values from Detournay and Cheng (1991), Eqs. 1–10 and p. 1655, as given to the agents.

2 · The solution

Drained at the tip, undrained at the crack scale

The crack-scale limits. A sudden load meets an undrained solid, so the opening is the Griffith ellipse with νu and the volume is π(1 − νu)L²/G. The change in mean stress raises the pore pressure next to the faces to 2(νu − ν)/[α(1 − 2ν)]; on the faces themselves p = 0 is prescribed. At long times the solid is drained: the volume is π(1 − ν)L²/G and the intensity under unit traction is the Griffith value √(πL), which contains no material constant.

The tip. The requested intensity is taken at the tip at fixed positive time, and then t → 0⁺. The induced pore pressure has unbounded gradients at a permeable tip, so within r ≪ √(ct) the tip is drained, however small t is. The drained tip field has to be matched to the undrained crack-scale field through the diffusion layer. In the Laplace domain this is a 2 × 2 Wiener–Hopf problem whose symbol depends only on ηt = (νu − ν)/(1 − νu), here 1/3. Its factorization gives K₁(0⁺) = 0.807593√(πL).

The paper’s simplified solution, 0.75 = 1/(1 + ηt), puts the undrained opening into the drained intensity formula; in the factorization this sets the matching factor n11 to 1. Taking t → 0 first at fixed r gives the outer value 1 instead. For gels (νu = 0.5), Yu et al. (2020) write the tip limit as [(1 − νu)/(1 − ν)]m with m ≈ 0.735 (their α, not the Biot coefficient); for this material the factorization corresponds to m ≈ 0.743 (our reading).

The volume growth. A reciprocal identity with the undrained slit solution turns the volume change into an integral of the fluid content against that solution’s stress trace. Along the faces, one-dimensional drainage layers give the paper’s term, 1.354055. Near each tip the drained region shrinks like √(ct), but the trace is singular there, so the tips contribute at the same √t order: 1.086165, from the same factorization. The coefficient is A = 2.440220.

Loading 2. A step in face pore pressure moves nothing at t = 0⁺, so K₂(0⁺) = 0. At long times the pressurized region around the crack is still confined by the infinite solid around it. It carries a uniform confining stress −η, which acts on the faces as a back-stress and closes the crack.

The tip calculation, step by step

The idea

After a time t, fluid near the tip has drained over a distance of about √(ct); farther away it has not had time to move. The tip therefore sits in a small drained region, which is more compliant, inside an undrained solid, which is stiffer. The calculation solves the problem of this transition region and compares the crack opening at its two ends, just behind the tip and far behind it. That ratio, corrected for the change in compliance, is K₁(0⁺)/√(πL).

The work is done in the Laplace transform of time, with variable s, where the drained distance becomes δ = √(c/s); small times are large s.

1. Reduce everything to the crack line

By symmetry only the upper half-plane y > 0 is needed, and everything that matters lies on its edge, the crack line y = 0. A Fourier transform along the line (wavenumber ξ, k = |ξ|) solves the equations off the line exactly. It leaves four quantities on the line: the opening ŵ, the normal stress Ŝ = σ̂yy, the pore pressure p̂₀, and the pressure gradient across the line Q̂ = ∂p̂/∂y, so that the fluid flux across it is −κQ. Their exact relations are in the box; C is the drained stiffness of the line, and b and β are coupling constants.

On the crack the faces carry the unit pressure and are drained: S = −1 and p₀ = 0. Ahead of the tip, symmetry gives no opening and no flux, w = 0 and Q = 0, while the stress and the pressure there are unknown. The kind of condition switches at the tip, and that switch is what makes the problem hard.

The bracket carries the physics. For wavelengths much longer than δ (k ≪ √(s/c)) the fraction (m − k)/(m + k) tends to 1, and the stiffness is C(1 + ηt) = G/[2(1 − νu)], the undrained value. For wavelengths much shorter than δ it tends to 0, and the stiffness is C, the drained value. Long waves are the crack scale; short waves are the tip.

Codex’s review, Eq. (1): VERDICT.md, lines 30–100; the algebra is checked symbolically in analytic_checks.py. The two limits of the bracket follow from the equation itself.

2. Zoom into the tip

Near a tip at small times the only length is δ. Distance from the tip is measured in units of δ, X = (x − L)/δ, and wavenumbers in units of 1/δ; from here on ξ, k and m are these scaled quantities, so m = √(1 + ξ²). Far behind the tip the opening must join the undrained crack field, which opens as the square root of the distance; it is normalized to √(−X) there.

The relations of step 1 then become one matrix equation. A 2 × 2 matrix N(ξ), in the box, acts on the opening and the flux, which are nonzero only on the crack (X < 0), and gives the stress and the pressure, which are unknown only ahead of the tip (X > 0), once known scalar factors are taken out. N depends only on ηt.

Its two ends are the two solids of step 1: N(0) = diag(1 + ηt, 1) is the undrained solid, at wavelengths long compared with δ, and N(∞) = I is the drained solid, at short ones. The off-diagonal terms, proportional to √(2ηt), couple deformation and flow; they vanish at both ends.

VERDICT.md, lines 123–151 (Eq. 4); the same matrix is built in wilson_factor.py, lines 21–26.

3. Split the matrix: the Wiener–Hopf method

An equation whose conditions switch at a point is solved by splitting its matrix into two factors, N = N₊N₋, one belonging to each side of the tip. In mathematical terms, each factor and its inverse are analytic in one half of the complex ξ-plane; the split exists because N is Hermitian and positive definite, like a stiffness.

The equation can then be rearranged so that one side contains only quantities on the crack and the other only quantities ahead of it. Both sides must equal the same simple function, which gives the solution without computing the fields everywhere. Requiring both factors to become the identity at short wavelengths, the drained tip, makes the split unique.

N₋ at zero wavenumber, the undrained far field, gives two numbers (box).

n₁₁ is the amplitude of the opening just behind the tip, w ≈ n₁₁√(−X), when the opening far behind is √(−X). n₂₁ is the amplitude of the pore pressure around the tip, p ≈ −√(2/ηt) n₂₁ √R cos(θ/2), where R (in units of δ) and θ are polar coordinates around the tip, with θ measured from the line ahead of it.

VERDICT.md, lines 151–184 (Eq. 5); wilson_factor.py, lines 47–54 (the normalization at short wavelengths, and n₁₁ and n₂₁ read at zero wavenumber).

4. Compare the two openings: K

Far behind the tip the opening is the undrained near-tip opening with the crack-scale intensity Kout = √(πL); at the tip it is the drained one with Ktip (r is the distance behind the tip). By step 3 the tip amplitude is n₁₁ times the far one, which gives K (box).

If the tip carried the crack-scale intensity, the opening amplitude would rise by the compliance ratio, 1 + ηt = 4/3. It rises only by n₁₁ = 1.077, so the intensity at the tip drops to 1.077/(4/3) = 0.808 of √(πL). The tip problem contains no s, so the same factor holds at every small positive time: it is K₁(0⁺).

VERDICT.md, lines 105–118 (Eq. 3) and 186–198 (Eq. 6).

5. Two checks that could fail

An energy balance. The transformed problem has a path-independent integral, like the J-integral, which includes a term for the energy carried by fluid flow. Evaluated at the tip and far from it, it requires the balance in the box: what flows in from the crack scale is split between elastic energy at the tip and flow. It is a balance in the transformed problem, not of energy in time. The computed values satisfy it to 7 × 10⁻¹⁴, with 13.04% in the flow term. Without the flow term, n₁₁ would be √(1 + ηt) and Ktip/Kout = 1/√(1 + ηt) = 0.866, the elastic-energy estimate of section 3.

A hand solution at weak coupling. When ηt is small, the pressure near the tip is known in closed form (box). It is zero at the tip and on the faces, and becomes the undrained singular pressure beyond the drained distance √(ct). Its amplitude gives n₂₁, and the energy balance then gives n₁₁ and K. The paper’s simplified solution gives 1 − ηt instead.

This expansion does not use the numerical factorization, so the two could disagree. They agree: the factorization’s slope, (Ktip/Kout − 1)/ηt, is −0.737 at ηt = 0.02 and −0.743 at 0.01 (rerun of 6 November), and −0.74993 at 10⁻⁴ (Codex), approaching −3/4.

VERDICT.md, lines 200–235 (Eq. 7) and 318–359 (Eq. 12 and the pressure in physical time); recomputed in tip_derivation_check.txt.

6. The tip term of the volume

The Derivation box writes the volume change as an integral of the fluid content against Ta, the stress trace σxx + σyy of the undrained slit solution. Along the faces, fluid drains in layers about δ thick, which gives the paper’s term. Near each tip the drained region is only about δ across, an area δ², which looks negligible.

But both factors are singular there: Ta ≈ √(2L/r) cos(θ/2), and the undrained pressure, which drains there, has the same 1/√r singularity. Each is of size δ−1/2 at distances of order δ, so their product over the area δ² is of order δ, the same order as the face layers. The box computes it.

In the scaled variables of step 2, with Z proportional to the fluid content and f = R−1/2 cos(θ/2) the shape of Ta, Green’s identity, an integration by parts in two dimensions, turns the area integral into the pressure amplitude of step 3:

With the scales restored and two tips, this is the tip term of A in the Derivation box: 1.086165, beside 1.354055 from the faces.

Numbers, and how to reproduce them

The command in the box factorizes N on three frequency grids, of 16,384, 65,536 and 262,144 points, and extrapolates: n₁₁ = 1.0767907 and n₂₁ = 0.4169595, so K₁(0⁺)/√(πL) = 0.807593 and A = 1.354055 + 1.086165 = 2.440220. These are numerical estimates, not certified bounds.

Separately, finite elements give K₁(0⁺) = 0.80 ± 0.01, and a direct finite-crack solve gives the tip term as 1.09 ± 0.01. The review was given GPT’s answers and code, so it is an informed check, not a blind one.

3 · What the solution says

The tip carries less than the crack, and the crack keeps opening

Just after loading the crack as a whole is stiffer than it will be: its volume starts at 1.885 and grows to 2.513 as the solid drains. The tip does not follow the crack. Its intensity starts at 0.81 of the Griffith value and rises to it as the drained region spreads.

The drop at the tip depends only on how different the drained and undrained solids are, measured by ηt. It lies between two simpler estimates. Matching elastic energy alone gives 1/√(1 + ηt), which is too high because part of the energy flux into the tip is carried by the pore-pressure field. The paper’s simplified solution gives 1/(1 + ηt); it leaves out the matching layer and falls below the full solution. At ηt = 1 the factorization gives 0.6019, against 0.5 from the simplified solution.

Loading 2 behaves differently. At long times the pore pressure near the crack tends to the face value, but only within a region of radius about √(ct) that keeps growing; beyond it the solid is unpressurized and holds the region in, like a heated inclusion in a cold plate. In two dimensions there is no bounded steady state, and the limits t → ∞ and r → ∞ do not commute. That confinement puts the region in compression, which closes the crack: K₂(∞) = −0.33375√(πL) and V₂(∞) = −0.839 L²/G. In a real fluid-filled crack this closure is superposed on loading 1.

# Problem: a suddenly pressurized crack in a fluid-saturated porous solid

An infinite, homogeneous, isotropic, fluid-saturated porous solid deforms in plane strain
(x–y plane). A crack occupies −L ≤ x ≤ L, y = 0. The solid obeys Biot's linear poroelasticity, with
shear modulus G, drained Poisson's ratio ν, undrained Poisson's ratio ν_u (ν < ν_u ≤ ½), Biot
coefficient α (0 < α ≤ 1) and permeability coefficient κ.

In terms of the total stress σ_ij (tension positive), the pore pressure p, the strain e_ij and the
variation of fluid content per unit volume ζ:

- 2G e_ij = σ_ij − ν σ_kk δ_ij + α(1 − 2ν) δ_ij p
- 2G ζ = α(1 − 2ν) σ_kk + α²(1 − 2ν)² p / (ν_u − ν)
- equilibrium: σ_ij,j = 0
- Darcy: q_i = −κ p,i
- mass balance: ∂ζ/∂t + q_i,i = 0

(indices 1, 2; summation implied). The diffusivity is c = 2κG(1 − ν)(ν_u − ν) / [α²(1 − 2ν)²(1 − ν_u)].

Stresses and pore pressure are zero everywhere for t < 0 and vanish at infinity. Two loadings act on
both crack faces (−L ≤ x ≤ L) for t > 0. Here σ_n = σ_yy is the normal stress on the faces and H is
the unit step.

- **Loading 1:** σ_n = −H(t), p = 0.
- **Loading 2:** σ_n = 0, p = H(t).

Let K be the mode I stress intensity factor at the crack tips, and V the crack volume per unit
thickness (the integral of the opening over the crack; positive for an open crack).

(a) For loading 1, find K and V just after loading (t = 0⁺) and at long times (t → ∞). Find also the
leading-order growth of V at small times, ct/L² ≪ 1.

(b) For loading 2, find K at t = 0⁺ and as t → ∞, and V as t → ∞.

(c) Evaluate your results for ν = 0.2, ν_u = 0.4 and α = 0.89.

**Report in `results.json`** (for (c); K in units of √(πL), V in units of L²/G, per unit loading
magnitude):
- `K1_0`, `K1_inf`, `V1_0`, `V1_inf`: loading 1 at t = 0⁺ and t → ∞;
- `V1_short_coeff`: the coefficient A in V ≈ V1_0 + A √(ct/L²) for loading 1 at small times;
- `p_faces_0`: in loading 1, the pore pressure induced in the solid immediately next to the crack
  faces at t = 0⁺, before it diffuses;
- `K2_0`, `K2_inf`, `V2_inf`: loading 2;
- `formulas`: your general expressions for all of the above, as strings.

5 · GPT-6.1 Sol

A transform formulation that kept both limits

All three GPT runs wrote the problem in Fourier–Laplace form, where drained and undrained behaviour appear as the two ends of one transform symbol. MP-37 then defined the tip intensity explicitly, “first extract the stress singularity at each positive time, then let time tend to zero”, and noted that the opposite order gives a different number.

It solved the tip problem by a canonical Wiener–Hopf factorization and checked the factors with the identity n11² + n21² = 1 + ηt, a Laplace-domain balance of the configurational energy flux that includes its pore-pressure part (not conservation of elastic energy during the transient). Reciprocity then gave the full volume coefficient, faces and tips: 2.440220.

MP-38 reached the same values. MP-39 found the tip value, 0.807, from a collocation calculation it called provisional, but kept the faces-only volume term 1.354. Its answer names the unresolved tip matching as “the principal missing part of this answer”.

A later extension to other materials showed the limit of the delivered code: at ηt = 1 its factorization returned an exponent near 1.15, inconsistent with the trend, where the separate Wilson factorization gives K/K₀ = 0.601850. The original success did not establish a solver that works for every material.

6 · Opus 5.5 · added trial

A coupled solver, with both orders of limits stated

Opus formulated the total-traction and pore-pressure boundary data as a Laplace-domain mixed problem, solved it with a Chebyshev–Galerkin method and inverted in time numerically. Its answer opens with the distinction the other agents missed: at t = 0 exactly the crack-scale value is √(πL), but for any t > 0 the tip is drained and K jumps to 0.8076√(πL), then rises.

It split the √t volume growth into the face layers, 1.354, and the tips, 1.086, and rejected a simpler “uniaxial swelling” estimate of 1.128 because it ignores the response of the body outside the layer; that is the value Kimi K3.1 high reported. For loading 2 it obtained the confined back-stress −η and noted that the long-time limits are approached only logarithmically.

Its tip intensity and tip volume term are numerical boundary-value results, reported as 0.807593 ± 0.000005 and 1.0862 ± 0.0003. Grok reran representative solver points, checked an energy identity and refitted the saved extrapolation, and graded all requested parts correct. It did not rederive every Fourier symbol or repeat the full fine-grid campaign.

7 · Kimi K3 and K3.1

The crack scale right, the tip and loading 2 not

The three K3 runs started, correctly, from undrained elasticity at t = 0⁺ and drained elasticity at long times, and gave √(πL) for the tip at both. All three noticed the tip zones of size √(ct) and dismissed them: MP-24 wrote that they “contribute only O((ct)3/4) and are subleading”. That estimate keeps the singular stress but treats the fluid content in the zone as bounded. It is singular too, as r−1/2; over a zone of area ct the two singular factors give order √(ct), the same as the faces.

For loading 2 all three took the long-time pore pressure to be uniform and the solid free to swell, which gives zero intensity and zero volume. In an infinite solid with zero pressure at infinity the pressurized region stays confined.

K3.1 Pro, in fresh high and max sessions on 24 October, kept the elastic tip value. High integrated the strain through the face layers and reported A = 1.128; max added the reciprocal response of the outer body and found the full face term, 1.354055, but not the tips. Both replaced loading 2 by an effective face traction α on a drained crack, giving −α in place of −η. Their own checks restate those reduced formulas, so they pass.

8 · GLM-5.3

Loading 2 right once; the tip treated as elastic

All three GLM runs gave K = √(πL) at both ends. MP-06 gave the reason: the intensity of a uniformly loaded crack is fixed by the tractions alone, for any Poisson ratio. It estimated the tip corrections to the volume as O(ct/L²) and kept a one-dimensional face-layer coefficient, 1.128.

For loading 2, MP-06 reasoned as the paper does. It described the pressurized matrix near the crack as “constrained by the far field, exactly like a hot inclusion which squeezes itself”, and obtained −η and the closing volume. The other two runs gave zero long-time intensity.

MP-06’s own numerical calculation produced K near 0.82, close to the corrected key. The record traced that number to the grid smoothing the singular pressure before any diffusion step; no refinement was supplied to tell smoothing from a converged tip limit. It was not evidence that the run had resolved drainage, and its stated answer remained 1.

9 · DeepSeek, reasoning on

An elastic tip and a reduced loading 2

The three reasoning-on DeepSeek runs reported the crack-scale value 1 at the tip. MP-46 gave an explicit reason: with p = 0 on the faces, it argued, the coupling term that could make K time dependent vanishes, so the total stress, and K, are constant in time.

That argument holds at the crack scale and fails inside the diffusion layer. MP-46 also ran a transient finite-element model and read its constant drained-modulus K as confirmation. By its own calibration the model was off by 34% in volume and 43% in K, with about 40 nodes along the crack; it could not resolve a tip layer of size √(ct).

For loading 2, MP-41 and MP-50 gave zero; MP-41 took the pressure to leak away to zero at every fixed point, and MP-50 took the long-time pressure to be a steady harmonic field, which it argued produces no stress. MP-46 applied the effective traction −α from the start, so its K₂(0⁺) was −0.89 instead of 0.

12 · Lessons

What this case study teaches, and what it does not

Each lesson rests on a handful of runs of one problem: observations to test on new problems, not measured rates.

Domain knowledge

A short time is short only at some scales

ct/L² ≪ 1 makes the crack undrained; r/√(ct) → 0 makes the tip drained. Eleven of the fifteen runs in the comparison used the first group alone at the tip, and none of them distinguished the tip from the crack-scale field.

Section 10 table; dimensional analysis.

Domain knowledge

Small regions near a singularity are not negligible

Several answers saw the √(ct) tip zones and judged their contribution to the volume higher order, O(t3/4) or O(t). Both the stress and the fluid content are singular there, which makes it O(t1/2), the same order as the faces.

MP-24, MP-22 and MP-06 answers.

Domain knowledge

Confined is not free

A uniformly pressurized body swells freely; a pressurized region inside an infinite solid is held in compression. Free swelling gave zero and effective traction gave −α; the confined region gives −η.

MP-06 answer; key part (b).

Verification

Checks that encode the assumption pass

K3.1’s verification scripts restated the effective-traction model and passed. A check can only catch an error it does not share.

Grok’s review of K3.1.

Verification

A number near the answer is not a solution

MP-06’s 0.82 came from grid smoothing before any diffusion step. Without refinement, an apparent confirmation needed more evidence.

Correction P382.

Running the study

An agent’s challenge can correct the key

The first key took the paper’s simplified value, 0.75, as exact, and the automatic comparison marked GPT’s full solution wrong. The key changed only after two factorizations and a finite-element check agreed, and it keeps the superseded values.

GPT challenge record.

Running the study

Write the order of limits into the problem

“t = 0⁺” admitted two readings. The next version of the problem states that the tip limit is taken first.

Key, “Order of limits”.

What the study does not show: that one model is better than another, that longer time or a different harness caused Opus’s result, or that the drained-tip result is new. Each would need matched, repeated runs, or a fuller literature comparison.
