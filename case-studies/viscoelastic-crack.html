Fracture mechanics case study · AI agents and a viscoelastic crack · 22–23 October 2026

Steady crack growth in a viscoelastic strip: can AI agents select the physical state?

Read the mechanics: model and solutionSee how the agents did

Hui and Long (2026) solve a crack that runs at constant speed along the middle of a viscoelastic strip, opening through a cohesive zone. We gave the problem, without the paper, to AI agents. A load, a zone length and the fields have to satisfy several conditions at once; the agents differed in whether the state they reported satisfied all of them.

Aim

To see whether agents close a partial reduction into a state that meets every physical condition, find the range of speeds where such a state exists, and verify the state rather than a residual.

Teng Zhang · pilot of 22 October 2026 with diagnostics of 23 October. Three solver submissions: GPT-6.1 Sol and two DeepSeek V4.1 Flash runs, one through Azure and one through DeepSeek’s own API. Two Kimi attempts delivered no solution. GPT’s submission has a completed external Grok review; automatic scoring of GPT was withheld because the key organizer shares its model family. No expert has reviewed this note.

Our question
Given the strip, the creep law, the free energy and the cohesive law, can an AI agent find the load, zone length and fields of a steadily moving crack, the range of speeds where it exists, and the energy dissipated in the bulk, and show that the state satisfies every condition?
Posed on 22 October 2026 with a six-hour budget. The paper and the reference were withheld; analytical or numerical methods were allowed.
The answer and its present limit

GPT rebuilt the published analytical solution in 17 minutes: the stress field, the load–zone relation that keeps the front stress bounded, the critical-separation equation and the terminal speed V* = 13.42. All 251 scorer checks passed, and Grok’s source-based review supported its four core derivations.

Both DeepSeek runs produced useful reductions and reproducible numbers but did not close them into the physical state. The Azure run’s zone lengths were off by 3.8–5.9% because a fixed-window fit biased the front condition. The direct run inferred a terminal speed 1/λ = 10 from an analyticity argument and dropped the requested case V = 10, which in fact has a finite steady state.

Appendix B
What the agents did · what separated them · why it matters

What the agents did

All three reduced the bulk to a harmonic problem with a history convolution. GPT wrote the stress field in closed form; the DeepSeek runs solved for the fields numerically.

What separated them

Selecting the state: imposing a bounded front exactly rather than through a biased fit, and finding the speed limit from the separation condition rather than from where a factorization breaks down.

Why it matters

A small residual of the solved equation, and an energy balance used to pick the state, both looked like verification. Neither showed that the reported state met every defining condition.

1 · The model

A cohesive crack running through material with memory

An infinite strip of height 2h deforms in antiplane shear: the only displacement w is out of the plane. Its edges are held at w = ±Δ. A crack runs along the midline at constant speed v; in the frame moving with it the fields are steady. Ahead of the front the interface is intact; behind it a cohesive zone of unknown length lc carries a constant shear traction τc until the opening 2w reaches δc; behind that the crack is traction free.

The material is a standard linear solid: a spring G∞ in parallel with a Maxwell branch, so it responds with the instantaneous modulus G₀ to fast loading and relaxes to G∞. A material point passing the crack feels a load history whose rate depends on the speed. The supplied free energy fixes how much of the work is dissipated locally in the bulk.

For a given speed the task asks for the edge displacement Δ, the zone length, the stress and strain fields, the energy released per unit advance and the bulk dissipation, at V = 0.001, 0.1 and 10, together with the range of speeds that admit a steady state.

2 · The solution

A bounded front fixes the load; the critical opening fixes the zone

The stress field. In this steady problem the stress is curl free as well as divergence free, so Hui and Long write it as one analytic function T(Z) of the complex coordinate, on the branch analytic in the upper half strip. Far ahead it gives the relaxed stress a = G∞Δ/(τch).

The bounded front fixes the load. A general solution has a square-root stress singularity at the front of the cohesive zone. Requiring the stress to stay bounded there ties the load to the zone length: a = (2/π) arccos(e−ε/2). The energy released per unit advance is then Γ = (a²/d)Γ0, so R = Γ/Γ0 = a²/d.

Memory enters through the opening. The strain at a point is a weighted history of the stress it has passed through, with weight e−u over the creep time. Integrating the shear strain γ1 = w,x across the cohesive zone gives the displacement there; setting the jump 2w(−lc, 0⁺) equal to δc gives one equation for ε at each speed (Eq. 51). It has a root for every speed below V*, where 1 − d = (1 − λ)k(V*) (Eq. 59); for λ = 0.1 and d = 0.2, V* = 13.4235715733. At V* the zone length diverges and R → 5: the endpoint is a limit, not a state with a finite zone.

Bulk dissipation. With the supplied free energy, the local dissipation is |T − E|²/(1 − λ) in normalized form. Its integral over the strip, divided by πdV, equals R − 1, the energy released beyond what the cohesive zone absorbs.

3 · What the solution says

Faster cracks need longer zones and release more energy, up to a terminal speed

At slow speeds the material has time to relax: the zone length approaches its relaxed elastic value 0.540 and the released energy equals the intrinsic fracture energy. As the speed rises the material ahead of the crack responds more stiffly, the cohesive zone lengthens and more energy is released, the excess dissipated in the bulk. At V = 10 the released energy is 4.74 times the intrinsic value.

The zone length diverges at V* = 13.42. That speed depends on the critical opening as well as on the modulus ratio: at fixed λ = 0.1, d = 0.15 gives V* ≈ 28.8 and d = 0.3 gives V* ≈ 5.76. A cutoff that depends on λ alone, such as 1/λ = 10, cannot be right.

Consequence of the solution The endpoint is a limit of the quasistatic steady model; it does not establish a dynamic instability.

4 · The test

The problem the agents received

The problem states the strip, the boundary and interface conditions, the creep law, the free energy and the Dugdale law, and asks for the steady branch, its speed range and limits, the scalars and field samples at three speeds, and a spatially integrated bulk dissipation with checks. Any analytical or numerical method was allowed. It does not give the stress field, the load–zone relation or the endpoint equation.

Each run had six hours. A frozen scorer compared 251 items: 193 values against the reference, three energy-balance checks and 55 checks of counts, uniqueness and admissibility. A supplementary 70-point analytical weighting was added after GPT had delivered, while the DeepSeek runs were still working; no solver saw it.

What was asked, abridged

1. The relation between load and speed, the zone length and fields; the admissible speed range, any endpoint and its limit; the slow-speed limit.

2. ε, a and R at V = 0.001, 0.1 and 10, and normalized stress and strain samples.

3. Bulk dissipation by spatial integration of τiγ̇i − ψ̇, with refinement, tail and energy-balance checks; subtracting Γ₀ from Γ does not count.

4. Checks of equilibrium, the material law, the interface and boundaries, and critical separation.

Problem as given · process log · grading status

Automatic scoring of GPT was withheld because the key organizer, Codex, shares GPT’s model family; Grok replayed the frozen scorer and reviewed the derivation against the paper. Three Grok attempts to grade the Azure DeepSeek run did not deliver a final grade, so its analytical score is absent.

5 · GPT-6.1 Sol

The published construction, analytic in the stress

GPT showed that the stress is curl free and wrote it as an analytic function of the strip coordinate, with the far-ahead relaxed state fixing its value. It removed the front singularity explicitly, obtaining a = (2/π) arccos(e−ε/2), then wrote the creep history as a convolution and the critical-separation condition as an equation for ε. It solved for the endpoint from the separation condition, not from the stress representation, and stated that the endpoint is a supremum rather than a finite-zone state.

Its code evaluates integrals and roots of those representations by quadrature; analytical recovery here does not mean an elementary closed form for every number. All 251 scorer checks passed, including 180 field components. Grok’s review supported the stress and boundedness construction, the causal creep history, the separation and loading equation, and the speed range with its limits, and gave 100/100 under both rubrics.

6 · DeepSeek via Azure

Right reductions, a biased front condition

The Azure run derived the standard-solid overstress equation, excluded a growing homogeneous mode far ahead, reduced the displacement problem to Laplace’s equation and wrote the history as a convolution. These are reusable steps of the solution.

To impose the bounded front it fitted the computed stress near the front to K/⁠√X + b + c⁠√X over a fixed window 0.02 < X < 0.4 and set the fitted K to zero. Higher regular terms, left out of that basis, leak into the fitted K, so a zero fitted K is not a bounded front. Its zone lengths came out 5.9% high at V = 0.001 and 3.8% at V = 0.1, and its loads missed the necessary relation a = (2/π) arccos(e−ε/2) by 2.45% and 1.52%.

A diagnostic on 23 October applied the same fit, at the same window and front-panel coordinates, to the exact continuum fields and changed only the selection condition. It reproduced about 96% and 98% of the submitted zone-length discrepancies. Adding four regular powers to the basis reduced the diagnostic error from 5.68% to 0.008% at V = 0.001.

7 · DeepSeek direct

An endpoint from the wrong condition

The direct run also reduced the bulk to a harmonic problem and derived an interface kernel. It then argued that the factorization of its transform symbol stays analytic only while a viscoelastic pole lies below the strip edge, which fails at V = 1/λ = 10, and concluded that no steady state exists at or above that speed. It marked the requested case V = 10 inadmissible and returned no values for it.

The endpoint is set by the separation condition, 1 − d = (1 − λ)k(V*), which couples the modulus ratio to the critical opening and gives 13.42 here. Of its 31 failed checks, 22 concern the V = 10 case and one the endpoint itself; 60 requested field comparisons were missing. They record one consequential decision, not 31 independent errors.

Once energy balance helped choose the state, its agreement with energy balance was weak evidence.

8 · What separated them

Where the runs diverged

All three runs reached the same bulk problem. GPT then imposed each defining condition exactly; the DeepSeek runs imposed the front condition and the speed limit through numerical or structural proxies. The Azure run’s bias can be reproduced from its fitting choice alone.

10 · Lessons

What this case study teaches, and what it does not

Each lesson rests on three submissions of one problem: observations to test on new problems, not measured rates.

Proof organization

Impose a condition, do not fit it

Setting a fitted singular coefficient to zero is not a bounded front when the fit basis is incomplete. The exact relation a(ε) was available as a check.

Azure solve.py; exact-field controls.

Domain knowledge

Existence comes from the physics, not the transform

Where a factorization stops being analytic is a property of the method. The steady branch ends where the separation condition can no longer be met.

Direct ANSWER.md; Eq. 59.

Verification

A balance used to choose a state cannot also confirm it

The direct run selected low-speed states partly by energy closure, then cited energy closure as a check.

Direct ANSWER.md, sections 4 and 7.

Verification

Small residuals of one equation are not a verified state

A 10⁻⁸ Laplace residual coexisted with constitutive residuals of order 0.3 and a biased front.

11 · Sources and scope

Study basis

One problem, three assessable submissions and two attempts without output: a case study, not a ranking. The reference reproduces the published solution; Codex implemented it; its scalars, V* and field samples were recomputed independently, and its dissipation was checked against R − 1. No expert has reviewed this note.

The agents’ words quoted here come from files they saved: GPT’s ANSWER.md, the Azure run’s solve.py and the direct run’s ANSWER.md. Private reasoning was not used.

Appendix B · Execution records

Execution records

Kimi. Two attempts ended in output-limit and execution failures with no mathematics saved. That is a delivery boundary for those attempts, not evidence that Kimi cannot solve the model. No GLM run was made in this pilot.

Reviews. Three Grok attempts on the Azure DeepSeek submission delivered no final grade. The two that could use tools made useful reads and checks; the third, given a fixed packet with its tools disabled, also timed out. An absent reviewer grade is not a failed solver score.

How the runs ended

GPT: finished at 17 minutes.

DeepSeek via Azure: finished at 3 h 49 min.

DeepSeek direct: finished at 2 h 58 min.

Kimi: two attempts, no solution.

Timing. The direct DeepSeek answer reports about six hours of work; host timestamps record 2 h 58 min. The discrepancy is recorded without inference about intent.
