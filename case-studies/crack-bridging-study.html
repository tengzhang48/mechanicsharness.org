1 · The problem

T1, as the agents received it

A finite flaw under opening tension

An infinite isotropic plate in plane strain contains a straight, traction-free slit on y = 0, from x = −a to x = a, with a = 2.40 mm. A uniform remote tensile stress Σ acts normal to the slit. The solid has

E = 130 GPa, ν = 0.28.

As the two crack fronts advance along the slit line, intact connectors remain between the separated faces on a < |x| < a + L. Their closing normal traction under monotonic opening is

t(δ) = 160 MPa (0 < δ < 9.00 µm),

and becomes zero irreversibly when the opening first reaches δc = 9.00 µm. Here δ is the full face-to-face gap, and L is the added crack length at each end. Advancing either outer crack front also consumes an intrinsic work Γ0 = 22.0 J/m², separate from connector work. Area means area of the crack plane, counting a pair of new faces once. Treat the intrinsic front process as spatially negligible. The bulk outside the separated region is linear elastic; assume symmetric, quasistatic growth and no other damage.

Following the monotonic branch from crack initiation, find:

  1. The remote stress Σ, in MPa, when a connector adjacent to the original slit first fails.
  2. The corresponding L, in mm per end.

Give a brief method and one physical or numerical check. Each numerical answer must be within ±0.3% of the reference. Budget: 45 minutes; Python with NumPy and SciPy is available.

The task file task/T1/TASK_PROBLEM.md, with its formulas set as text here; each run also received a short header with file conventions (a full task file).

2 · Hints, one at a time

From the picture to the deciding relation

Hint 1 The picture: small-scale or large-scale bridging?

Bridging replaces the details of a fracture process by a law. Behind the crack tip, connectors such as fibres or ductile ligaments pull the faces together with a traction t(δ) that falls to zero at a limiting gap δc. The law has a strength scale σb and a gap scale δc, and with the elastic modulus they define one material length, E′δc/σb. Advancing the tip itself costs a separate intrinsic work Γ0, such as the matrix fracture energy.

When the bridged zone is much shorter than the crack and the body, bridging is small-scale: the zone sits in the square-root field and the body enters only through K. […] When the crack or the component is comparable to the zone, bridging is large-scale: the zone feels the geometry, toughness and R-curves stop being material properties, and the load has to be computed from the body and the law together.

For T1, E′ = E/(1 − ν²) gives a material length of 7.93 mm, so a/(E′δc/σb) = 0.30. Which regime is it, and what does that mean for using a toughness?

Case page, Section 1 (text) and its opening figure, panel A.

Hint 2 Two conditions hold at once

In both problems the state at first failure is fixed by two conditions that hold together. At the front the crack advances quasistatically, so the energy reaching the tip equals Γ0. At the other end of the bridged zone, the oldest connector, which has opened most, reaches δc. The load and the bridged length are the two unknowns.

Case page, Section 2.

Hint 3 Superposition, and the condition at the front

By linearity the crack of half-length c = a + L carries a pressure Σ on the slit and Σ − σb on the bridged zones.

Its tip intensity is

Ktip = Σ√(πc) − 2σb√(c/π) arccos(a/c),

and the front condition, for an opening crack the positive root Ktip = +√(E′Γ0), gives the load for any bridged length:

Σ = (2σb/π) arccos(a/c) + K0/√(πc), K0 = √(E′Γ0).

The key’s script, key/T1/reference.py (paper’s Eq. 46).

Hint 4 The deciding relation: the opening at the slit root

The oldest connector sits at x = a. What is the full opening there, for a crack of half-length c under these pressures?

One way, as GLM built it: the opening of a strip-yield crack under the stress that gives zero tip intensity, plus the opening of an open crack under the remaining stress K0/√(πc). With u = ln(c/a),

δ(a) = (8σba/πE′) u + 4K0√(c² − a²)/(E′√(πc)).

Setting δ(a) = δc and using hint 3 leaves one equation for u:

4k√(2 sinh u) + 8u/π = E′δc/(σba), k = K0/(σb√(πa)).

Case page, Section 6 (GLM’s construction, “Eq. 45 in another form”); the equation for u is the key’s, reference.py, analytic_solution.

Hint 5 An independent route: the crack-opening Green function

To test an opening formula without trusting hint 4, build the opening from a kernel. For a crack |s| < c with symmetric face pressure p(s), the full opening at x is

δ(x) = (4c/πE′) ∫01 p(ct) [G(r, t) + G(r, −t)] dt, r = x/c,

G(r, t) = ln{[1 − rt + √((1 − r²)(1 − t²))] / |r − t|}.

A uniform pressure gives the ellipse 4p√(c² − x²)/E′. For the closing traction on the bridged wings, the integral at r = a/c has a closed form:

∫r1 [G(r, t) + G(r, −t)] dt = 2√(1 − r²) arccos r + 2r ln r.

Use it to derive the root opening for the pressures of hint 3. Which term of hint 4 comes from the connectors?

The key’s independent route, reference.py, green_kernel and opening_by_green; the closed form for the wings is checked numerically against that kernel by this page’s tests.

Hint 6 Checks before you trust it
  • No front work, Γ0 = 0: ln(c/a) = πE′δc/(8σba), the paper’s strength formula (Eq. 47).
  • No connectors: a uniformly pressurized crack opens as an ellipse, 4p√(c² − x²)/E′.
  • A long crack, a/(E′δc/σb) → ∞ with the material fixed: L tends to the small-scale zone of Eq. 20 and Σ to the toughness estimate √(E′(Γ0 + σbδc)/(πa)).
  • The branch, for the T1 inputs: from initiation at 20.29 MPa, Σ and the root opening should both rise up to the failure.

The key’s checks, reference.py, main: the first two, and the long-crack stress with Γ0 = 0. The long-crack limits with Γ0 > 0 are checked by this page’s tests; the branch, by the case page, Section 2.

Answer The key

Σ = 133.7303 MPa and L = 4.0856 mm per end, recomputed by three routes. At first failure the bridged zone is 1.70 times the original half-slit. Replacing the connectors by the plateau toughness Γ0 + σbδc = 1462 J/m² on the original slit gives 165.4 MPa, 24% above the coupled answer.

Case page, Section 3 and Section 8.

4 · Two agents at the deciding step

One run derived the opening; the other wrote a wrong one

Every run set up the coupled problem and kept the front work apart from the connector work. On the slit the runs differed in one relation, the opening at the root of the partly bridged crack.

Without the knowledge unit about the paper, GLM-5.3 and DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (reasoning off) each wrote a root opening. Their two answers are on the right, as they wrote them.

Your turn

One of the two openings is wrong. Test both on two cases whose answer you know: a crack with no connectors (t = 0), and a strip-yield crack loaded so that the tip intensity is zero (Σ = ΣD), taking c = 2a. Which test catches the error, and which does not? In GLM’s form, K0/√(πc) is the stress beyond ΣD: for the first test it equals Σ, for the second K0 = 0.

Answer What went wrong, and how a check could have caught it

DeepSeek on the slit, with reasoning off, wrote the tip intensity correctly but took the root opening as (4/E′)[Σ√(c² − a²) − t(c − a)], which is not the opening of a crack under that pressure. With it, the root connector reaches δc only at L = 34.3 mm and Σ = 158.5 MPa, 740% and 18.5% above the key. Eq. 45 gives an opening of 24.3 µm at that state, so on the actual path the root connector had failed long before. Its opening “check” evaluated a double integral that, at x = a, reproduces the same t(c − a) term. It scored 47: no points for the numbers, and full verification credit for a correct, independent check of the tip intensity.

The two tests: with t = 0 both formulas give the ellipse, so that test passes the wrong one. At the strip-yield state with c = 2a, ΣD = 2t/3 and √(c² − a²) = √3 a; the correct root opening is (8ta/πE′) ln 2 = 1.765 ta/E′, while DeepSeek’s formula gives 0.619 ta/E′. Only a test that involves the connectors can see an error in the connectors’ term.

GLM on the slit built the root opening itself (hint 4). It checked the first part against a dislocation-density integration, to 6 × 10⁻¹¹, and solved the whole problem again by a separate spectral method, 133.70 MPa and 4.079 mm. Graded 100.

5 · What to take away

Two lessons from this case

At a/(E′δc/σb) ≈ 0.3 a toughness does not apply

The zone at first failure is 1.7 times the half-slit, and the toughness on the original slit overestimates the load by 24%. The finite geometry has to be solved with the law.

A check that reuses the formula cannot test it

DeepSeek’s opening check without the unit integrated an expression that reduces to its own wrong term at x = a, so it confirmed the error.

Case page, Section 9. Each lesson rests on eight runs of two agents on two problems: observations to test on new problems, not measured rates.
