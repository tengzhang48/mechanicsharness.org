A bridged crack: when does the first connector fail?

Fibres or ligaments that bridge a crack hold its faces together. You will find the load at which the first of them breaks, see why a toughness cannot give that load here, and then find the error in a formula that an AI agent wrote for the same problem.

You will practise
  • deciding whether bridging can be condensed into a toughness;
  • coupling a bridging law to a finite crack through two conditions;
  • choosing a check that can actually catch a wrong formula.
You need
  • the stress intensity factor and superposition of crack-face loads;
  • the strip-yield (Dugdale) crack;
  • pen and paper, and something to solve one equation numerically.

1 The problem

A large plate in plane strain contains a slit of half-length a. A remote tension Σ pulls it open. As the load rises the crack grows at both ends, and connectors stay behind across the new crack faces, over a length L at each end. Each connector pulls the faces together with a constant traction σb until the gap between the faces reaches δc; then it breaks for good. Moving the crack front itself costs a work Γ0 per unit area, separate from the connectors.

Half-length of the slita2.40 mm
Young’s modulus, Poisson’s ratioE, ν130 GPa, 0.28
Connector tractionσb160 MPa
Gap at which a connector breaksδc9.00 µm (full gap, face to face)
Work to advance the frontΓ022.0 J/m²

Find the remote stress Σ at which the first connector breaks (the one next to the original slit), and the bridged length L at each end at that moment.

Try it on paper first. An answer counts as right within 0.3%, the tolerance the AI agents were given. If you get stuck, the steps below take you there.

The task exactly as the AI agents received it

A finite flaw under opening tension. An infinite isotropic plate in plane strain contains a straight, traction-free slit on y = 0, from x = −a to x = a, with a = 2.40 mm. A uniform remote tensile stress Σ acts normal to the slit. The solid has E = 130 GPa, ν = 0.28.

As the two crack fronts advance along the slit line, intact connectors remain between the separated faces on a < |x| < a + L. Their closing normal traction under monotonic opening is t(δ) = 160 MPa (0 < δ < 9.00 µm), and becomes zero irreversibly when the opening first reaches δc = 9.00 µm. Here δ is the full face-to-face gap, and L is the added crack length at each end. Advancing either outer crack front also consumes an intrinsic work Γ0 = 22.0 J/m², separate from connector work. Area means area of the crack plane, counting a pair of new faces once. Treat the intrinsic front process as spatially negligible. The bulk outside the separated region is linear elastic; assume symmetric, quasistatic growth and no other damage.

Following the monotonic branch from crack initiation, find: (1) the remote stress Σ, in MPa, when a connector adjacent to the original slit first fails; (2) the corresponding L, in mm per end.

Give a brief method and one physical or numerical check. Each numerical answer must be within ±0.3% of the reference. Budget: 45 minutes; Python with NumPy and SciPy is available.

2 The physical picture

Before any formula, watch what happens as the load rises. Drag the slider, or press play. The opening is drawn about 160 times larger than the lengths along the crack.

The oldest connector is stretched to of its breaking gap

No connectors yet: the crack has not grown.

  1. The slit opens. At low load nothing grows: the slit opens elastically, widest in the middle.
  2. The crack starts to grow. The fronts need only the small work Γ0 to advance, so growth starts early, far below the final load.
  3. Connectors stay behind and pull back. Each new piece of crack is still held by connectors. They carry part of the load and shield the fronts. In this problem that means the load must keep rising for the crack to keep growing, all the way to the first failure: the growth is stable.
  4. The oldest connector is stretched most. It sits where the original slit ended, and it has been stretching ever since the crack passed it. When its gap reaches δc, it breaks. That is the moment the problem asks about.

So the answer needs two things at once: how much load keeps the fronts moving (the tip, step 3), and how far the oldest connector has opened (the root, step 4). Notice also how long the bridged zones have become by the end: comparable with the slit itself. That is why a single toughness number cannot describe this crack (step 1).

The slit opens elastically. Nothing grows yet. The fronts advance. Connectors stay behind across the new crack and stretch. The oldest connector has reached its breaking gap and fails.

What happened

Test 1 passes both formulas. Test 2 separates them: the correct root opening is (8/π) ln 2 = 1.765 ta/E′, and B’s formula gives 0.619 ta/E′. Only a test that involves the connectors can see an error in the connectors’ term.

Agent B was DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (run with reasoning off). From the case study:

DeepSeek on the slit, with reasoning off, wrote the tip intensity correctly but took the root opening as (4/E′)[Σ√(c² − a²) − t(c − a)], which is not the opening of a crack under that pressure. With it, the root connector reaches δc only at L = 34.3 mm and Σ = 158.5 MPa, 740% and 18.5% above the key. Eq. 45 gives an opening of 24.3 µm at that state, so on the actual path the root connector had failed long before. Its opening “check” evaluated a double integral that, at x = a, reproduces the same t(c − a) term. It scored 47: no points for the numbers, and full verification credit for a correct, independent check of the tip intensity.

Agent A was GLM-5.3.

GLM on the slit built the root opening itself: the opening of a strip-yield crack under the stress that gives zero tip intensity, plus the opening of an open crack under the remaining stress K0/√(πc). That is Eq. 45 in another form. It checked the first part against a dislocation-density integration, to 6 × 10⁻¹¹, and solved the whole problem again by a separate spectral method, 133.70 MPa and 4.079 mm. Graded 100.

“The key” is the reference answer and “Eq. 45” the source paper’s root opening. 6 Take away

A toughness needs a short zone

Here the zone at first failure is 1.7 times the half-slit, and the toughness on the original slit overestimates the load by 24%. The finite geometry has to be solved with the law.

A check that reuses the formula cannot test it

DeepSeek’s opening check integrated an expression that reduces to its own wrong term at x = a, so it confirmed the error. Pick a check that exercises the term you doubt.

These rest on eight runs of two agents on two problems: observations to test on new problems, not measured rates. The full case study has the second problem, all runs and the grading.
