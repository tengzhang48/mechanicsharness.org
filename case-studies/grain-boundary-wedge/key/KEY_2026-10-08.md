## Reference answer

**(a) The traction decays as a sum of eigenmodes.**
- σ_gb = σ₀ Σ cₙ e^(−λₙ t/t₀) fₙ(z/h) (Eq. 11), with the eigenproblem
  λₙfₙ(x) = −∫₀¹ S(x,ξ) fₙ‴(ξ) dξ and fₙ(0) = 0, fₙ′(1) = fₙ″(1) = 0 (Eqs. 13, 15).
- **The eigenvalues** (Table 1, p. 2869; two methods agree):
  - λ₁ = **11.74265**;
  - λ₂ = **264.6348**;
  - then 1357.44, 3845.2 and 8328.1.
- **The first mode dominates:** "the diffusion process is for the most part dominated by the first
  eigenmode" (p. 2869).
- **Reproduced (ours):** the paper's Gauss–Chebyshev collocation (Appendix A, Eqs. A1–A8), with N =
  60 and 120, gives λ₁ = 11.742662 and λ₂ = 264.635.
- **A small inconsistency in the source's text.** It says "the second eigenmode decays 25 times
  faster … the fourth nearly 400 times … the fifth nearly 1000 times" (p. 2869). Table 1's ratios
  are 22.5, 115.6, 327 and 709 (ours).
- **The paper's own closed-form approximation** (Appendix B):
  - the first mode taken as a mode I crack shape, f₁″ = −3√(1 − x²) (Eq. B6);
  - λ₁ ≈ 3π/∫₀¹f₁ dx = **13.3595** (Eq. B8), "within 15% of the exact value";
  - reproduced (ours).
- **mode1_shape_half** (ours, from the reproduced first eigenvector): f₁(0.5)/f₁(1) = **0.8020**.
  This agrees with Fig. 4a and Fig. B1; Eq. B6 gives 0.811.

**(b) A crack-like wedge.**
- "the diffusion process leads to the formation of crack-like grain-boundary wedges which cause the
  normal traction along the grain boundary to decay exponentially with time" (abstract).
- "the stress intensity factor rapidly approaches the value associated with a traction-free edge
  crack on a time-scale of order t₀/λ₁" (p. 2870; Fig. 7, K̂ → about 1.1).
- **The value of the edge-crack limit is not printed in the paper.**
  - The standard result for an edge crack of depth h in a half-space under uniform tension is
    1.1215 σ₀√(πh). That is a handbook value, not from this paper.
  - Our solution of the traction-free limit with the paper's kernel converges towards it: 1.117,
    1.119 and 1.120 at N = 30, 60 and 120 (ours).
- **Grading band:** 1.09–1.15.

**(c) The relaxation time is of order t₀/λ₁ ≈ 0.085 t₀:** "Complete relaxation of the grain-boundary
traction occurs on a time-scale of order t₀/λ₁" (p. 2869). Grading band: 0.05–0.15 t₀ for
`relaxation_time_t0`.

## Grading (our choices)

**Automatic layer** (`checks.py`):
- λ₁ within 1% (the exact solution) or within 15% (an approximate route such as Eq. B8), recorded as
  two classes;
- λ₂ within 2%;
- mode1_shape_half within 2% of 0.802;
- K_inf_norm in 1.09–1.15;
- relaxation_time_t0 in 0.05–0.15;
- **principle checks on the agent's own numbers:**
  - λ₂ > λ₁;
  - the relaxation time agrees with its own 1/λ₁ within a factor of 2.

**Grader layer:**
- the formulation (deriving the eigenproblem; the role of each boundary condition);
- the method (numerical, or the paper's approximate mode I route) and its verification;
- every error, classified; checks able to fail; source naming.

## Lessons this problem probes

- **L2:** the exact eigenvalues need a careful numerical solution of a singular integro-differential
  eigenproblem. This is the round's long problem.
- **L3:** the right method, a singular-integral collocation, or the crack-shape approximation for an
  estimate.
- **L5:** checks that could fail (convergence, two methods).
- **L10:** the boundary conditions, free surface against interface.
- **L8:** source naming.
