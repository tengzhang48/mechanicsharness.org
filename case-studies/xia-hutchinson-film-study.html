A guided fracture mechanics exercise. Find the energy released by channel cracks in a stretched film, then test a face condition.

How much energy does a new crack between two old ones release?

A crack that runs through a stretched film and channels across it releases a fixed energy per unit advance, once it is longer than a few film thicknesses. That energy does not depend on the crack’s length: it is the energy of the film far ahead of the advancing edge minus the energy far behind it. When a new crack runs midway between two old ones, the film it cuts has already lost part of its stress. You will find how much less the new crack releases, and how much more stress the next set of cracks needs.

You will practise
  • finding an energy release rate from the states far ahead and far behind, without a crack-tip field;
  • solving a one-dimensional strip problem with the right condition on its faces;
  • turning an energy release rate into the stress needed to grow the next set of cracks.
You need
  • plane stress, the energy release rate and mode I from the introductory page;
  • the hyperbolic functions sinh, cosh and tanh, and a linear second-order differential equation;
  • pen and paper, and a calculator. Every number on this page can be found by hand.

1 The problem

A film bonded to a thick substrate is often stretched. For example, a film deposited hot and then cooled is left in tension when it would shrink more than the substrate. Here the film carries the same tension σ0 in every in-plane direction before any crack forms: an equibiaxial prestress. A crack can run through the film’s thickness and spread across it, like a crack in dried mud or in a glaze. Such a crack is called a channel crack.

The model. The paper replaces the substrate by a linear foundation. The film, of thickness h, carries plane-stress membrane forces, and the substrate pulls back on it with a force −k u per unit area. A bed of springs is a picture of this foundation: k is the spring stiffness per unit area of film. The disturbance caused by a crack decays over a distance of order ℓ, the decay length (the paper’s Eqs. 4 to 6 and 12):

Greek indices take the values 1 and 2, and a repeated index is summed. uα is the in-plane displacement measured from the prestressed state, εαβ its strain, and Δσαβ the stress change it causes. E and ν are the film’s Young’s modulus and Poisson’s ratio, and nβ is the unit normal to a crack face. See plane stress and the sign conventions.

Choosing k. The paper chooses k so that one isolated crack in the model releases the same energy as a steady channel crack in the full film and substrate, as computed by Beuth (1992). This gives ℓ = (π/2)gh (the paper’s Eqs. 1 and 10 to 12). Here g is Beuth’s dimensionless function of the elastic mismatch between film and substrate, plotted in the paper’s Fig. 1. Without mismatch, ℓ = 1.98h (the paper, p. 1113). You will not need this calibration below: every answer is given in units built from ℓ.

ℓ is the decay length defined above. Gc is the film’s toughness: the energy per unit area of new crack that the film needs in order to crack. Their numerical values are not needed for the dimensionless answers.

Two crack histories. The coordinate x1 runs along the cracks, and the tips advance in the +x1 direction; x2 runs across the cracks. In case A, straight cracks a distance H apart all advance together into intact film. In case B, old cracks a distance 2H apart already run through the whole film. New cracks advance midway between them, and the old cracks stay as they are. Case B is an idealized picture of sequential cracking, in which each new set of cracks forms between the cracks of the set before. In both cases the cracks far behind the tips end up a distance H apart.

The crack faces. Before cracking the film carries an equibiaxial tension σ₀. Cracks run through the thickness, and their faces are free of total traction: the prestress plus the stress change, not the change alone. On a face along x1, this means σ22 = σ0 + Δσ22 = 0 and σ12 = 0. Here σ22 is the stress across the cracks and σ11 the stress along them.

Energy and stress intensity. G is the energy released per advancing tip, per unit area of new crack: the release per unit advance of one tip, divided by the film thickness h. The stress intensity factors KI and KII are the conventional plane-stress ones. See the energy release rate and the stress intensity factor. Energy release G is energy per advancing crack area: divide release per unit in-plane advance per tip by h. It has units J/m2. Two crack geometries

Case A consists of semi-infinite cracks on x2 = mH, x1 < 0, for every integer m, with H > 0. Each tip is at x1 = 0. The displacement increment tends to zero far ahead, and the relaxed cracked wake is reached far behind. The field is periodic in x2 with period H. All tips advance an equal distance in the positive x1 direction.

Case B already contains infinite cracks on x2 = 2mH for all x1. Additional semi-infinite cracks lie on x2 = (2m + 1)H, x1 < 0, with tips at x1 = 0. Far ahead, the film is in the equilibrium state of the existing infinite cracks. Far behind, the additional cracks split that wake. The field is periodic in x2 with period 2H. Only the additional tips advance, equally in the positive x1 direction; the old cracks remain fixed.

Every face of every crack is traction free. For each case the far-ahead and far-behind states are the bounded equilibria of the stated model.

The same after the new crack, in a strip of width H:

  1. The faces carry no stress. A crack face is free of total traction, so σ22 = 0 at each face. There the crack has released the whole prestress across it.
  2. The stress recovers over about ℓ. Away from a face, the foundation resists the film’s displacement. So the stress across the cracks builds up again, toward σ0, over a distance of about ℓ.
  3. Narrow strips never recover. In a strip narrower than about ℓ, the stress across the cracks stays low everywhere. A new crack in such a strip has little stress to release, so it releases little energy.
  4. The stress along the cracks remains. The stress σ11 along the cracks is lowest at the faces, where it is (1 − ν)σ0. Everywhere in a strip it stays at least (1 − ν)σ0.

Step 4 Case A: all cracks advance together

From the case study:

In steady state the crack pattern simply translates. When every advancing tip moves forward by a unit length, the net effect is to move one unit of film from the state far ahead of the tips to the state far behind them; the complicated region near the tips is carried along unchanged. The energy released per tip is therefore the energy per unit length of the ahead state minus that of the behind state.

5 Find the error

Two agents solved this problem with the same first steps. Both wrote the strip equation u2″ = u2/ℓ2 of step 2 and its hyperbolic solutions. They differ in the condition they imposed on the crack faces far behind the tips. Agent A set the total stress across the crack to zero. Agent B set the change of that stress from the prestressed state to zero, and both coefficients of its solution vanished. Agent B then put the prestress back, but tried only fields linear across the strip, and again found only u = 0. It concluded that no relaxed state exists far behind the tips.

First, the physics

First: which stress must vanish on a free crack face?

Test 1 · the traction on a face

Evaluate the total stress across the crack, σ22 = σ0 + Eu2′, on a face in each agent’s far-behind state. In Agent A’s state, Eu2′ = −σ0 at the faces, so σ22 = 0 there. In Agent B’s state u = 0, so σ22 = σ0 at the face: the face carries the full prestress.

Which agent’s far-behind state has traction-free faces?

Test 2 · an isolated crack

Let the cracks be very far apart, so that each behaves as an isolated crack. Step 1 says that an isolated crack releases G = σ02ℓ/E. If the state far behind were Agent B’s u = 0, its energy change would be U = 0, the same as the intact film far ahead. The count far ahead minus far behind would then give G = 0 at every spacing, even for an isolated crack. Agent B did not report G = 0: its numbers came from a separate finite-element calculation. That calculation also gave its own field far behind the tips, 18ℓ behind them at W = ℓ. There σ22 was about 0.003σ0 at the faces and 0.114σ0 at the centre. The strip solution of step 2 gives zero and about 0.113σ0.

Whose far-behind state agrees with the isolated crack and with Agent B’s own finite-element field?

Your verdict. Which agent wrote a face condition that the cracked film does not satisfy?

What happened

A free crack face carries no total traction, and only Agent A’s far-behind state passes both tests. Agent B’s condition, Δσ22 = 0 at the faces, leaves the full prestress on them. Its state u = 0 would release no energy, even from an isolated crack.

Agent B’s numbers came from another route. Close numbers from a separate route, finite elements, did not reveal the wrong argument, because the numbers never passed through the wrong face condition.

6 Take away

The observed errors involved the prestress

With σ = σ₀ + Δσ, the prestress enters the face condition and the work term of the energy. DeepSeek’s default run left σ₀ out of the face condition; its later run wrote that the work term vanishes, when it is twice the strip energy. Grok’s own control shows the effect of the first error: with only the stress change set to zero at the faces, u = 0 and γ = 0. On this page, the face condition is σ0 + Eu2′ = 0 of step 2, and the work term is the prestress term of step 3.

A restricted trial became a general claim

DeepSeek’s default run tried only fields linear across the strip, found none, and concluded that no relaxed state exists, four minutes after writing the hyperbolic solutions of the same second-order equation. The strip equation u2″ = u2/ℓ2 has no linear solution except u2 = 0, so a search among linear fields could not find the relaxed state.

Values and grades do not show whether the written argument is right

Close values hid a wrong argument in DeepSeek’s default run; exact values came with two written errors in its later run. Grok noticed one of those errors and still rated the energy item right, because the code was right. The written argument needs its own check against the code. Test 2 of Section 5 is such a check: it follows the written argument to a case with a known answer, the isolated crack.

These lessons are based on a few runs of one problem. Treat them as ideas to test on new problems, not as measured error rates. The full case study also has Kimi’s run, a later DeepSeek run with thinking switched on, whose written answer has two errors that its code does not, and all the grading.
