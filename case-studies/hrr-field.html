Fracture mechanics case study · AI agents and nonlinear crack tips · runs of 6–7 October 2026, written up 25 October

Crack and notch tips in a hardening material: can AI agents rebuild the HRR field and adapt it?

Read the mechanics: model and solutionSee how the agents did

Hutchinson (1968) found the singular field at a tensile crack in a power-law hardening material: the stress exponent 1/(n+1), the angular shape from a nonlinear eigenproblem, and the amplitude from Rice’s J integral through a number In. An appendix applied the method to a V-notch. We gave four problems built from the paper, without the paper itself, to AI agents: its plane-strain crack at n = 6.5, a 120° notch, an antiplane crack and a bilinear material. Every answer that reported a crack exponent had it right. Three answers missed most of their numbers: two lost a term of the J integral, one of them after solving a wrong angular problem, and one wrote wrong equilibrium equations for the notch; two runs did not finish.

Aim

To see whether agents can solve a nonlinear angular eigenproblem, normalize it by J without losing a term, and recognize that a crack result does not carry over to a notch.

Teng Zhang · probe round of 6 October and matched test of 7 October 2026. Probe round: GLM-5.3 and DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, one run per problem with and without a knowledge unit on the paper (8 runs, 6 answers). Matched test: two runs each of GLM-5.3 and DeepSeek per problem in three knowledge conditions (24 runs); Kimi K3 was launched 16 times on V1 and V2; the token-rate limit stopped 13 launches, and it left three complete, ungraded V1 answers and one draft. DeepSeek ran through Azure with reasoning off in the probe round and through its own API with reasoning on in the matched test. GPT and Opus were not run as solvers. Partially blind grades by Codex; reference values recomputed independently. No expert has reviewed this note.

Our question
Given a power-law hardening law and a sharp crack or notch, can an AI agent find the stress exponent, solve the angular eigenproblem, fix the amplitude by J, and adapt the analysis to a notch, to antiplane shear and to a material with a finite tangent slope?
Posed in two rounds, on 6 and 7 October 2026: 32 runs with 30 graded answers, and 16 Kimi K3 launches, each with a 45-minute budget and a 50-minute limit. The paper itself and the keys were withheld.
The answer and its present limit

Mostly yes. Of 30 graded answers, 26 had every requested number within tolerance, and all three Kimi K3 answers matched the antiplane key in our comparison. The crack exponent was right in every answer that reported one. The failures were elsewhere: two DeepSeek runs without knowledge lost a term of the J integral to a parity error, on T1 and V1 (on T1 after solving a wrong angular problem), and one wrote a wrong radial exponent into the notch’s equilibrium equations.

The limit is the design. The probe round had one run per cell and the matched test two, and the matched problems sat at the ceiling for both solvers, so the runs do not measure a knowledge effect. DeepSeek’s reasoning setting and provider differed between the rounds, and two GLM runs reached the time limit with no answer.

What the agents did · what separated them · why it matters

What the agents did

Every answer used a separable power-law field: an Airy function (in one answer, displacements) with an angular eigenproblem in plane strain, a scalar angular equation in antiplane shear, and the elastic r−1/2 field with modified constants for the bilinear material.

What separated them

The J integral and the governing equations, not the scaling. A dropped or mis-signed term changed the amplitude and every number after it while the exponent stayed right.

Why it matters

In both J failures the run’s second evaluation agreed with its first because both shared the error. Two evaluations that agree check nothing unless they differ in the step at risk.

1 · The model

Power-law hardening at a sharp crack or notch, in small strain

The material follows deformation-theory (J2) plasticity with power-law hardening: the strain is a power of the effective stress times the stress deviator, so plastic flow conserves volume. Deformation theory stands in for incremental plasticity only under proportional loading with no unloading, and the kinematics are infinitesimal. The crack is stationary and sharp, with traction-free faces, and is loaded symmetrically; J is the energy flux through a contour around the tip.

T1 is the paper’s own plane-strain problem at a hardening exponent its table does not list, n = 6.5, with the fully plastic law alone. T2 keeps elasticity plus hardening, at n = 4, but replaces the crack by a 120° notch. The matched test changed the setting further. V1 loads the crack in antiplane shear with a pure power law in shear, n = 3.5, and a given J. V2 is a plane-stress membrane of a bilinear material whose tangent slope T must be inferred from a transverse-strain coefficient.

T1 as in the paper · T2, V1, V2 variants T1 follows Secs. 4–5 (Eqs. 24, 26, 27, 31, Table 1). T2 adapts the V-notch of the Appendix (p. 31), V1 the antiplane crack the paper cites (p. 19), and V2 the piecewise-linear law of Sec. 6 (pp. 24–25).

2 · The solution

J fixes the exponent and the amplitude of the crack field; at a notch an eigenvalue fixes the exponent

Which strain dominates. Approaching the tip the stress grows without bound, so for n > 1 the power-law strain overtakes the elastic strain: the leading field is that of the pure power law, and E and ν drop out of the exponent and the angular shape. An Airy stress function σ0K rs f(θ), with amplitude K, satisfies equilibrium; compatibility of the power-law strains gives a fourth-order nonlinear equation for f, the paper’s Eq. 31 in plane strain. Symmetry ahead of the tip and two free-face conditions give four conditions, so with f(0) = 1 both s and f″(0) are fixed: an eigenproblem.

The crack: J fixes the exponent. Stresses go as rs−2 and strains as rn(s−2), so the J integrand goes as r(n+1)(s−2)+1. A nonzero, path-independent J requires that power to vanish: s = (2n+1)/(n+1), stress ∝ r−1/(n+1) and strain energy density ∝ 1/r (Eq. 26). The eigenproblem with s left free returns the same value.

J fixes the amplitude. J equals αε0σ0Kn+1 times an angular integral In (Eqs. 23, 24 and 27). In needs the whole contour integrand: the energy term and the work of both traction components, with the displacement derivatives taken in Cartesian components so that the rotation of the polar basis is included. Under symmetric loading each product in the integrand is even in θ. A product wrongly taken as odd gets dropped, since odd terms cancel over −π to π; R31 dropped σrθuθ,x this way, although both factors are odd and their product is even.

The notch. At a notch the faces are inclined, so J on a contour from face to face is no longer path independent and does not fix the exponent. The exponent comes from the same eigenproblem with the free faces at ±β. For T2, β = 2π/3 and n = 4, it is p = 0.1562, below the crack’s 0.2 and far from the elastic wedge’s 0.3843. The amplitude needs matching to the whole body, so T2 asked only for p and the ratio R.

Antiplane shear and the bilinear material. In antiplane shear the single displacement w obeys a second-order angular equation; the exponent is again 1/(n+1), and the dimensionless angular coefficient I of J has a closed form, π(n+1)/(2n) when f′(0) = 1. With a finite tangent slope T the leading strain is still linear in stress, so the tip field is the elastic r−1/2 field of an isotropic solid with modulus T and Poisson ratio νt: the deviatoric compliance changes while the bulk response stays elastic. The paper’s Sec. 6 finds the stresses reduced by √(Etan/E) (Eq. 42). In V2 the transverse-strain coefficient then fixes T.

Where the field holds. Each field is the leading term, as r → 0, of a small-strain solution without unloading. The paper warns that its singular fields are “only accurate representations of the solutions in a very small portion of the plastic zone” (p. 26). J fixes the exponent and the amplitude whenever J ≠ 0, also beyond small-scale yielding (p. 21). Small-scale yielding is needed only to compute J from the remote load through the elastic outer field (Eq. 27); otherwise J must come from a solution for the whole body. Blunting, finite strain and unloading are outside the model.

3 · What the solution says

Hardening weakens the singularity, and a notch weakens it further

At n = 6.5 the stress ahead of the crack grows only as r−2/15, while the strain grows as r−13/15; their product, the energy density, goes as 1/r for every n. The hoop stress ahead is σθθ = 1.894 σ0[J/(αε0σ0r)]2/15, and a disk of radius R stores 0.457 JR per unit thickness.

Because H depends on In only through the power 2/15, an error of about 2% in J hardly moves it, while C, proportional to 1/In, shows it. The audit’s answer built by interpolating Table 1 gets H within tolerance and misses the C tolerance.

A notch lowers the exponent further: at n = 4 from 0.2 to 0.156, and the lower the hardening exponent, the larger the gap (figure, a). Ahead of the 120° notch the radial stress is 0.373 of the hoop stress, against 0.793 ahead of a crack at the same n (our check).

4 · The test

Two rounds, four problems

Codex built both sets of problems from the paper alone. The solvers did not get the paper itself, though the probe round’s knowledge unit includes an extract of it. The probe round, on 6 October, gave T1 and T2 to GLM-5.3 through OpenCode and DeepSeek V4.1 Flash through Cline on Azure, each once with a frozen knowledge unit on the paper (an entry, an extract, a report, solver code and tests) and once without. DeepSeek ran without Cline’s thinking flag; its four session logs record no reasoning events (our count), the setting identified as a configuration error in the 8 October round.

The matched test, on 7 October, gave V1 and V2 to GLM-5.3 and DeepSeek through OpenCode, where both reasoned (our count of reasoning tokens), in three conditions: B0, a topic on crack-tip asymptotics with checks and a Hutchinson entry; B1, the same plus the entry’s reference code; C, nothing. Each cell had two repeats. Kimi K3 through Cline was set up for the same cells but hit the Azure token-rate limit: 16 launches on V1 and V2, three of them complete.

Every run had a 45-minute budget in its instructions and a 50-minute hard limit. Codex graded each answer against a 100-point rubric and reran the solver’s code where needed. Blinding was partial, since answers that had knowledge mention it, for example the three with-unit answers of the probe round. The rubric totals do not separate right from wrong (R31 scored 55 with two of three numbers wrong, R23 76 with both wrong), so this page gives the count of numbers within tolerance beside each grade. T1’s statement ends with a hint about rotating the polar basis, which the audit flags as a cue to the step that decides H and C.

What was asked, abridged

T1. Plane-strain crack, fully plastic law, n = 6.5: p, H and C = U(R)/(JR), with a brief method and one verification.

T2. 120° notch, elastic plus power law, n = 4: p and R, with a physical justification of the model and one verification.

V1. Antiplane crack, n = 3.5, J = 0.120 N/mm: face slip at two radii, signed stresses at one point, three radii of the |γ| = 0.01 contour; whether these fix initiation.

V2. Bilinear membrane, J = 0.800 N/mm, Czz(0) = −0.005 mm½: T, three strain coefficients, a displacement coefficient and a screened interval; whether initiation is fixed.

V2’s statement, kept as given, has three flaws (audit of V1 and V2, Section 1). It presents J and Czz as the output of “an external calculation” and “a computation”, although neither value is in the paper; they are the data of an inverse problem, and the mathematics is unaffected. And “evaluated using the leading field” can also be read as putting the leading stresses into the complete law, which moves the lower screen to 0.00927 mm. No graded answer used that reading; M22 considered it and set it aside (answer). It also stipulates that corrections to the leading field are negligible for r ≤ 0.200 mm, which is invented and which no answer needs; the complete law’s own σy term changes εzz by up to 17% at that radius.

5 · GLM-5.3

Right numbers in 13 of 14 answers; two probe runs ran out of time

In the probe round GLM finished two of four runs. On T1 with the unit (R11, 16 minutes) it used the unit’s solver and got p, H and C within tolerance, with In = 4.818379; its scripts could not be rerun outside its folder because they import the unit’s module (grade). On T2 without the unit (R09, 43 minutes) it wrote its own shooting solver, argued that the hardening term dominates near the tip, and got p and R within tolerance. Beside them it gave the elastic-wedge ratio as 0.987; the correct value is 0.444 (grade).

The other two probe runs, T1 without the unit and T2 with it, reached the 50-minute limit with no answer. On T2 its notch.py imported the unit’s crack solver and called a private function of it; the pilot summary records that the cause of the timeout is not established beyond that.

6 · DeepSeek, reasoning off · probe round

Right with the unit; without it, the J integral and the notch equations went wrong

With the unit both runs were right. On T1 (R35, 34 minutes) DeepSeek wrote its own code and took the J integral from the unit’s documented Eq. (24); all three numbers matched the key to 10⁻¹⁰. On T2 (R32, 21 minutes) it adapted the unit’s plane-strain equation to the notch, noting that “s is not fixed (as it is for a crack); it is the eigenvalue”, and chose the branch by continuation from the crack. It lost points for asserting, rather than showing, that hardening dominates.

Without the unit, on T1 (R31) it found p = 2/15 from the J scaling but solved a different angular problem. It imposed σrr = 0 instead of σθθ = 0 on the face, dropped the symmetry condition f‴(0) = 0, used a wrong shear compatibility relation, and dropped the shear-traction work σrθuθ,x from J as odd, although both factors are odd and the product is even. It reported H = 2.151 and C = 0.292 against 1.894 and 0.457. Its angular and Cartesian evaluations of J agreed at 7.7687; the grader found that both rested on the same wrong field and that the Cartesian check gave the shear stress the wrong parity, which repeated the dropped term. It wrote the answer before reaching the 50-minute limit.

7 · DeepSeek, reasoning on · matched test

Fast and right, except one parity slip in J

In the matched test DeepSeek answered all twelve runs in 2.5 to 7 minutes, and eleven had every number within tolerance, in all three conditions.

M20 (V1, no knowledge, second repeat) set up the antiplane problem correctly and found the exponent 2/9. Building the full-circle J, it extended f′ as odd, like f, instead of even. That cancelled a cross term: its J coefficient was 624.1 instead of 953.9, and every number shifted, the slips by 39%, the stresses by 10% and the contour radii by 53%. Its energy check recovered the input J with the same coefficient, so it could not see the error, and the answer stated that all seven numbers were within tolerance. 11 · Lessons

What this case study teaches, and what it does not

Each lesson rests on a few runs of four problems from one paper: observations to test on new problems, not measured rates.

Domain knowledge

The exponent is the easy part

Every reported crack exponent was 1/(n+1). The amplitude was where answers failed: R31 and M20 each lost a term of the J integral to a parity error, R31 on top of a wrong angular problem.

R31 and M20 grades.

Domain knowledge

A notch is a new eigenproblem

Neither the crack exponent, 0.2, nor the elastic wedge’s, 0.384, is right for the 120° notch at n = 4. R23’s equilibrium error vanished at n = 1, so its linear check could not see it.

R23 grade; audit mutants.

Verification

Two routes that share a step are one route

R31’s angular and Cartesian J agreed because both rested on the same wrong field and the Cartesian check repeated the parity error; M20’s energy check reused its own wrong coefficient.

R31 grade, unsupported claims; M20 grade, unsupported claims.

Verification

Check the quantity that is sensitive

H depends on In through the power 1/(n+1), so at n = 6.5 an error of up to about 2% in J passes its tolerance; C, proportional to 1/In, does not.

Audit, Section 3(d); mutant “table_interpolation”.

Running the study

Report the numbers, not only the total

R31 scored 55, R23 76 and M20 76 with their decisive numbers wrong; M20 had none of its seven within tolerance. The rubrics gave method credit that the totals do not separate from correctness.

Audit, Section 3, rubric audit; audit of V1 and V2, Section 3(e).

Running the study

Check the harness before reading the result

DeepSeek ran without reasoning in the probe round, GLM lost two runs to the time limit, and Kimi K3 13 of 16 launches to a token-rate limit. Each changes what a score means.

What the study does not show: how GPT or Opus would do as solvers, which they were not; whether the knowledge helps, since the cells are one or two runs and the matched problems sat at the ceiling; or how agents would do on a problem not already solved in the literature. The HRR field is classical, and recall of it cannot be excluded: the audit found that all five graded without-unit answers on T1–T3 name “HRR”.

12 · Sources and scope

Study basis

Four problems from one paper, 32 runs with 30 graded answers, and three ungraded Kimi answers: a case study, not a ranking. The keys and the grades come from AI models; the keys were recomputed by the benchmark audit and by our own script. No expert has reviewed them.

Two applicability problems built from the same paper are not reported. T3 of the probe round asked for recommendations on invented specimens with “measured” plastic zones, strains and J values, and the audit’s verdict is to restate it. A1 of the matched test is left out by our reading for the same reason: its cases rest on invented measurements, such as a “measured” volume strain of 0.018, and the statement lists the three verdicts to choose from.

The agents’ words quoted here come from files they saved: ANSWER.md of R09, R23, R32, R35, M08, M20, Kimi’s V1 B1 r1 run and its V1 C r1 draft, and code of R31 (graded copy) and M20. Session logs were used only to count event types and reasoning tokens; their content was not read.

Every file this page links is in the case package: the tasks, the keys, rubrics and checkers, every saved answer, the grades, and the records cited here. Session transcripts are not included.

Earlier records: probe pilot summary · matched-test summary · benchmark audit of T1–T3 · audit of V1 and V2 · excluded problems: T3, A1. A first summary of this case was drafted by GPT on 25 October in a separate review folder; this page replaces it.

Appendix B · Execution records

Execution records

DeepSeek’s reasoning. In the probe round DeepSeek was launched through Cline without its thinking flag; its four session logs hold only text and tool events, while a Kimi K3 log through the same client holds reasoning events. The same setting was identified as a configuration error on 8 October. In the matched test DeepSeek ran through OpenCode and used 19,942 to 53,941 reasoning tokens per run.

Interrupted attempts. Four DeepSeek probe runs were cut off when the Cline daemon was stopped during a disk-full incident and were rerun from rebuilt, hash-checked folders. The first attempts are kept as “.attempt1-hubkilled” and are not counted.

How the runs ended

Probe round: six of eight runs answered. Three reached the 50-minute limit, two of them, both GLM, with no answer. Matched test: all 24 GLM and DeepSeek runs ended normally with an answer, in 2.5 to 36 minutes. Kimi K3: 16 launches, three complete V1 answers, one draft and twelve without a file; each failed launch’s error record holds the token-rate-limit message.
