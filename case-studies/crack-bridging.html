Fracture mechanics case study · AI agents and crack bridging · 6 October 2026 probes, written up 25 October

A finite bridged crack: can AI agents couple the bridging law to the geometry?

Read the mechanics: model and solutionSee how the agents did

Bao and Suo (1992) showed when a bridging law, the traction that fibres or ligaments exert across a crack, can be condensed into a toughness and when it cannot: once the bridged zone is comparable to the crack or the component, the load has to be computed from the body and the law together. From their paper we posed two such problems, a finite bridged slit at new inputs and a variant with two unequal bridged strips, and gave each to GLM and DeepSeek with and without a knowledge unit about the paper.

Aim

To see whether agents keep the front work separate from the connector work, find the relation that couples the bridged zone to the finite geometry, and check the relation that decides the answer.

Teng Zhang · probes run on 6 October 2026. Eight runs on two tasks: GLM-5.3 and DeepSeek V4.1 Flash with reasoning off, each with and without the knowledge unit, one run per cell. GPT, Opus and Kimi were not run as solvers on this problem. Partially blind rubric grades by Codex; the reference numbers were recomputed by independent routes in the audit of 25 October and, from the stated formulas, by our own script for this page. No expert has reviewed this note.

Our question
Given a bridging law, an intrinsic front work and an elastic body, a finite slit in a plate or two unequal strips, can an AI agent find the load and the bridged length at which the first connector fails?
Posed to eight runs on 6 October 2026 with a 45-minute budget, four of them with a knowledge unit about the paper. The reference and rubric were withheld.
The answer and its present limit

Seven of the eight runs reported every requested number within tolerance. The exception was DeepSeek without the unit on the finite slit: it wrote a wrong opening for the partly bridged crack, reported 158.5 MPa and 34.3 mm per end against 133.73 MPa and 4.086 mm, and its opening checks reused that formula; only its check of the tip intensity was independent. With the unit, the same agent took the paper’s finite-crack relations from it and scored 100. GLM scored 100 on both tasks in both conditions; without the unit it derived the opening itself.

There is one run per cell and DeepSeek ran with reasoning off, so the contrast on the slit is one pair of runs, not an effect size. On the strip variant all four runs got the numbers within tolerance, with or without the unit; DeepSeek’s verification there scored 12 of 12 with the unit and 6 of 12 without (totals 100 and 94), one run each. The audit also found that its third number, δ(L/2), cannot detect a wrong arm stiffness. The with-unit answers name the paper, so their grading was only partially blind.

What the agents did

All eight runs set up a coupled problem: a front condition that keeps the intrinsic work Γ0 apart from the connector work, and failure of the oldest connector. On the strips every run added the two arm compliances; three took the couple from the energy balance, and GLM without the unit took it from the rear curvature and checked it against the balance.

One relation: the opening at the root of a partly bridged finite crack. Taken from the unit, or derived by GLM, it gave the key; DeepSeek’s version without the unit had the wrong form.

The wrong run still earned full verification credit for a correct check of the tip intensity, and its opening “check” integrated the same wrong expression. A check has to test the relation that decides the answer.

1 · The model

A traction law across the crack, coupled to an elastic body

Bridging replaces the details of a fracture process by a law. Behind the crack tip, connectors such as fibres or ductile ligaments pull the faces together with a traction t(δ) that falls to zero at a limiting gap δc. The law has a strength scale σb and a gap scale δc, and with the elastic modulus they define one material length, E′δc/σb. Advancing the tip itself costs a separate intrinsic work Γ0, such as the matrix fracture energy.

When the bridged zone is much shorter than the crack and the body, bridging is small-scale: the zone sits in the square-root field and the body enters only through K. A toughness then exists, Γ = Γ0 + ∫t dδ (Eq. 15, p. 359), the resistance rises along an R-curve to it, and, when the front work is small, Γ0 ≪ σbδc, the zone at the plateau is about 0.4 E′δc/σb long: (π/8)E′δc/σb for a constant traction (Eq. 11, p. 358) and 0.366 E′δc/σb for a linearly softening one (Eq. 28, p. 360). Front work shortens it: for a constant traction it is (π/8)(E′δc/σb)[⁠√(1 + g) + √g]−2 with g = Γ0/(σbδc) (Eq. 20, p. 359). Small-scale bridging needs this length to be much shorter than the crack and the body. With little front work that is the paper’s condition a/(E′δc/σb) ≫ 1 (Eq. 14, p. 358); front work shortens the zone and so relaxes the condition. When the crack or the component is comparable to the zone, bridging is large-scale: the zone feels the geometry, toughness and R-curves stop being material properties, and the load has to be computed from the body and the law together (Sec. 1.4, p. 358; Sec. 3, p. 360).

2 · The solution

Two conditions at once: the front moves and the oldest connector breaks

In both problems the state at first failure is fixed by two conditions that hold together. At the front the crack advances quasistatically, so the energy reaching the tip equals Γ0. At the other end of the bridged zone, the oldest connector, which has opened most, reaches δc. The load and the bridged length are the two unknowns.

The slit. By linearity the crack of half-length c = a + L carries a pressure Σ on the slit and Σ − σb on the bridged zones. The paper’s Eq. 46 gives the tip intensity and Eq. 45 the opening at the slit root, x = a, in closed form (from Tada et al., as cited on p. 362). Setting Ktip = √(E′Γ0) and δ(a) = δc and eliminating Σ leaves one equation for u = ln(c/a): L = 4.0856 mm per end and Σ = 133.7303 MPa. From initiation at 20.29 MPa to that point both Σ and the root opening rise, so the event lies on the monotonic branch. With Γ0 = 0 the same conditions give the paper’s strength formula, Eq. 47.

The strips. Each arm bends under the connector tractions, and the opening δ = w1 + w2 obeys δ⁗ = −C t(δ), where C = 1/D1 + 1/D2 adds the compliances of the two arms. At the front the faces meet with zero gap and slope, and the front work sets the tip curvature: the two tip couples release Cmtip²/2 = Γ0. At the cut root the gap is δc, the shear vanishes because there are no end forces, and the curvature carries the applied couple.

The J-integral, taken round the arms and along the bridged faces, gives the couple without the profile: Cm²/2 = Γ0 + σbδc/2, so M = 0.21263 N·m. The length and the profile need the beam solution. In units of ℓ = 6.41 mm the profile is a sum of hyperbolic and trigonometric functions; its rear conditions give a characteristic equation for λ = L/ℓ = 1.6844, hence L = 10.800 mm and δ(L/2) = 38.219 µm. For equal arms of thickness h, with D = Eh³/12 as T2 prescribes, the left side Cm²/2 becomes 12m²/(Eh³); the paper’s Eq. 51 has the same form with its plane-strain modulus E′ in place of E.

3 · What the solution says

In large-scale bridging the body sets the bridged length

The slit. At first failure the bridged zone is 1.70 times the original half-slit. The small-scale estimate of the zone at the plateau, Eq. 20 with this Γ0, is 2.43 mm, about a itself, so the premise of a toughness, a zone much shorter than the crack, does not hold here. Replacing the connectors by the plateau toughness Γ0 + σbδc = 1462 J/m² on the original slit gives 165.4 MPa, 24% above the coupled answer.

The front work moves the load little and the length a lot. Without it the same failure comes at 131.8 MPa and L = 6.39 mm: the load changes by 1.4%, the bridged length by 56%.

The strips. Energy fixes the couple and the beam fixes the length. Written as δ/δc against x/ℓ, the profile depends only on Γ0/(σbδc), and the arms enter through ℓ ∝ C−1/4. Averaging the two arms into one equal pair changes M to 0.266 N·m and L to 12.07 mm but leaves the midpoint opening at 38.22 µm; the opening 5 mm from the front moves from 31.8 to 24.3 µm. This is the paper’s point about the splitting beam in another form: its J–L curves depend on the beam thickness and are not material properties (p. 364).

Both answers hold inside their models: for T1, an infinite linear-elastic plate with symmetric quasistatic growth and a constant traction; for T2, the prescribed strip bending, in which the zone is 12 times the thicker arm and the rear rotation is 0.043 rad. The error of the strip model against two-dimensional elasticity was not estimated.

4 · The test

Two problems from the paper, with and without a knowledge unit

On 6 October Codex built three probes from the paper alone: T1, the paper’s method at new inputs; T2, a variant the paper does not solve; and T3, an applicability task that is not reported here. Each statement gives every input and convention, the full gap, the length per end, the area counted once, the couple on one strip, and names neither the paper nor the model. It asks for numbers within a tolerance, a brief method and one check.

Each probe went to GLM-5.3 through OpenCode and to DeepSeek V4.1 Flash through Cline via Azure, once with a knowledge unit about the paper in ./knowledge/ and once without: one run per cell. DeepSeek ran with reasoning off, as in the first multiphysics round: the launch command passed no thinking flag, and a count for this page found no reasoning events in its four logs. The unit, written by Kimi and frozen at 16:27 UTC on 6 October, the minute the run folders were built, holds a summary entry, an extraction of the paper’s equations including Eqs. 45–47, a report, a spectral solver for the finite bridged crack, and tests. The stated budget was 45 minutes, with a 50-minute hard limit.

What was asked, abridged

T1. Following the monotonic branch from crack initiation, find the remote stress Σ when a connector next to the original slit first fails, and the corresponding L per end, each within ±0.3%, with a brief method and one check.

T2. Increasing the couples quasistatically, find M on each strip, L and δ(L/2) when a newly retained connector first reaches δc, each within ±0.5%; justify the mechanical description and boundary conditions, and give one check.

Each answer was graded by a fresh Codex session against the rubric, the reference and its code; the graders reran the solvers’ code where numbers depended on it. The labels were blind, but the four with-unit answers name the paper or the unit, so for them the grading was partially blind. T1’s rubric gives 50 points to the two numbers and 50 to geometry, front work, inputs, verification and the event; T2’s gives 40 to the three numbers, 40 to the mechanics, 12 to verification and 8 to scope. No automatic check ran at the time; on 25 October the audit applied the rubrics’ tolerance bands to the numbers taken from the eight answers, and its verdicts agree with every grader’s numerical rows.

5 · With the knowledge unit · GLM and DeepSeek

The paper’s formulas, taken from the unit

The slit. Both runs used the finite-crack relations from the unit’s extraction, set Ktip = √(E′Γ0) and the root opening to δc, and reported 133.73 MPa and 4.086 mm. Both reported a check of the Γ0 = 0 limit against the paper’s Eq. 47. DeepSeek first ran a spectral solver that its script describes as “reused/extended from the knowledge unit”; it gave 133.6–133.9 MPa and 4.05–4.13 mm depending on the grid, and DeepSeek then found the closed forms in the unit. GLM checked the closed-form opening against its own quadrature of the crack-opening kernel. Its script also calls the unit’s spectral solver, which the grader could not run without the unit, and the spectral refinement sequence it reports is not in the supplied files.

The strips. Both runs added the two arm compliances, took the couple from the energy balance across the bridged zone, solved the beam for the length, and were graded 100. Their extra material had faults that the grades list without deducting points: a separate DeepSeek length search returned 48.7 mm, which the answer does not mention, and its continuation applied the softening law past δc; GLM wrote that the softening foundation gives “exponential rather than oscillatory modes”, whereas the characteristic roots ±μ and ±iμ give both.

6 · Without the knowledge unit · GLM and DeepSeek

One run derived the opening; the other wrote a wrong one

GLM on the slit built the root opening itself: the opening of a strip-yield crack under the stress that gives zero tip intensity, plus the opening of an open crack under the remaining stress K0/⁠√(πc). That is Eq. 45 in another form. It checked the first part against a dislocation-density integration, to 6 × 10⁻¹¹, and solved the whole problem again by a separate spectral method, 133.70 MPa and 4.079 mm. Graded 100.

DeepSeek on the slit, with reasoning off, wrote the tip intensity correctly but took the root opening as (4/E′)[Σ⁠√(c² − a²) − t(c − a)], which is not the opening of a crack under that pressure. With it, the root connector reaches δc only at L = 34.3 mm and Σ = 158.5 MPa, 740% and 18.5% above the key. Eq. 45 gives an opening of 24.3 µm at that state, so on the actual path the root connector had failed long before. Its opening “check” evaluated a double integral that, at x = a, reproduces the same t(c − a) term. It scored 47: no points for the numbers, and full verification credit for a correct, independent check of the tip intensity.

On the strips both runs got all three numbers. GLM finished in 9 minutes with the exact characteristic equation and executed energy and statics checks; its stated limits were wrong, since with Γ0 → 0 the root is 1.875, not π/2. DeepSeek’s prose put δ = δc at the front and δ = 0 at the cut root, the wrong way round; the grader found the calculation to be the mirror image of the right one and accepted it, but its checks were boundary residuals only: 6 of 12 for verification, 94 in total.

9 · Lessons

What this case study teaches, and what it does not

Each lesson rests on eight runs of two agents on two problems: observations to test on new problems, not measured rates.

Domain knowledge

At a/(E′δc/σb) ≈ 0.3 a toughness does not apply

The zone at first failure is 1.7 times the half-slit, and the toughness on the original slit overestimates the load by 24%. The finite geometry has to be solved with the law.

T1 reference diagnostics; check output, section 1; paper Eq. 14, p. 358.

Domain knowledge

Energy gives the couple, not the length

On the strips the curve area fixes M through Cm²/2 = Γ0 + σbδc/2; L and the profile need the beam solution with both arms. All four runs kept these apart.

T2 reference; grades R15, R21, R26 and R36, scope rows.

Verification

A check that reuses the formula cannot test it

DeepSeek’s opening check without the unit integrated an expression that reduces to its own wrong term at x = a, so it confirmed the error.

R03 grade, line 28.

Running the study

Give the points to the check of the deciding relation

R03 earned 15 of 15 for verification with both numbers wrong, because the rubric credits any executed elasticity check; its correct check tested the tip intensity, not the opening.

R03 grade, line 10; audit, Section 3(e).

Running the study

Ask for a number that sees the error

δ(L/2)/δc depends only on Γ0/(σbδc), so δ(L/2) passes averaged or one-arm stiffnesses; an opening at a fixed distance would not.

Audit, Section 3(d); check output, section 2.

What the study does not show: that a knowledge unit helps in general, how GPT, Opus or Kimi would do, or how DeepSeek with reasoning on would do. The finite-crack relations are classical, and recall of them cannot be excluded.

10 · Sources and scope

Study basis

One paper, two tasks, eight runs, one run per cell: a case study, not a ranking. The keys and the grades come from AI models; the keys were recomputed by independent routes, and the grades’ tolerance verdicts were rechecked by the audit. No expert has reviewed them.

The probe set also had an applicability task, T3, run four times, which is not reported here because it is a laboratory narrative with invented observations that hand over the decisive step, and because, in the audit’s reading, its key for case C goes beyond and partly against the paper, which says tunnelling cracks are resisted only by the matrix fracture energy (p. 364); the audit’s verdict on T3 is to restate it.

The agents’ words quoted here come from files they saved: ANSWER.md of P038-T1-with-glm, P038-T1-without-glm and P038-T1-without-dsaz, PROCESS.md and solve.py of P038-T1-with-dsaz, and ANSWER.md of P038-T2-with-glm. Private reasoning was not used. The paper is cited by page and equation; its text and page images are not included in this collection.

Every file this page links is in the case package: the tasks, the keys, rubrics and checker, every saved answer, the grades, and the records cited here. Session transcripts are not included.
