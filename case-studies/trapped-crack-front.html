Fracture mechanics case study · AI agents and a trapped crack front · Runs of 13 November 2026

A crack front trapped by tough bands under mixed loading: can AI agents find it when the theory is not given?

Read the mechanics: model and solutionSee how the agents did

Gao and Rice (1986) found how the three stress intensity factors change, to first order, when the front of a planar crack is slightly curved. We built a problem on that result: a crack front in equilibrium with periodic tough bands, loaded in tension and two shears. Four AI agents received the problem alone, with no paper, no formulas beyond the definitions and no web. One passed all 76 checks in under six minutes and has every piece of the physics: 100 points. The other three passed 3, 8 and 12 checks and score 20, 40 and 30 points. All four read the problem as intended. What separated them was one piece of mechanics: how the two shear modes respond to the shape of the front.

Aim

To see whether an agent given only a problem can supply the specialised result it rests on, by recall or by its own derivation, and whether its checks would catch a wrong one.

The numbers were checked by a program against a closed-form reference; the method notes were read by a second model (Grok 4.7) and by us. No expert has reviewed this note. All four set the problem up as intended: the tension response, the energy release rate, the load level and the Fourier solution of the equilibrium condition. Three wrote the response of the shear modes from memory; one computed it.

1 · The model

A planar crack on a weak plane with tough bands

A crack lies in the plane y = 0 of an infinite, isotropic, linear elastic solid with Poisson's ratio 0.3. It covers the half-plane x < a(z) and can extend only within that plane, which is weak. Its front is the curve x = a(z).

The solid is loaded from far away, and the loading does not change as the front moves: any straight front, wherever it lies, carries the same uniform stress intensity factors in tension and in the two shears, in the ratios 1 : 0.6 : 0.8. Along a curved front the two shear factors are taken in the local frame of the front, across it and along it.

The fracture energy of the weak plane varies along z only, with period λ: it is higher in bands centred at z = 0, ±λ, … and lower between them, with small contrast ε. The front is in equilibrium where the energy release rate equals the fracture energy at every point.

The task has two parts. Part A is a warm-up with a prescribed front, a single cosine of small amplitude: report the cosine and sine coefficients of the three stress intensity factors and of G. Part B is the equilibrium front in the bands: the load level, the front shape and the three factors at sixteen points of a period, and the largest shear along the front with its position. Everything is asked to first order.

Our configuration The body, the loading class and the first-order setting are those of Gao and Rice (1986). The tough bands under mixed loading are our own configuration; we have not searched the literature for it beyond the two papers named at the end, so we do not call it new.

2 · The solution

Where the front lags, tension rises; shear has its own weights and is coupled through the slope

Tension. Rice (1985) showed that a part of the front that lags behind its neighbours carries a higher K₁, and a part that leads carries a lower one. The change at a point depends on the whole front shape through a line operator 𝓛[a]; on a wavy front of wavenumber k it multiplies the wave by −|k|/2. This is why a tough band can hold a front back: the front lags there until the extra driving force balances the extra toughness.

Shear. Gao and Rice (1986) extended the result to the two shear modes. Two things change. The same operator appears with a weight that is different for each mode and depends on Poisson's ratio: (2 − 3ν)/(2 − ν) for shear across the front and (2 + ν)/(2 + ν − 2ν) = (2 + ν)/(2 − ν) for shear along it, both equal to 1 at ν = 0. And the two shear modes are coupled through the local slope of the front: tilting the front turns part of each shear into the other, with factors 2/(2 − ν) and 2(1 − ν)/(2 − ν). At ν = 0 these reduce to the plain rotation of the shear traction into the tilted frame; for ν > 0 they do not.

Energy. In the energy release rate the two slope terms cancel exactly. This is not an accident of the formulas. G is the derivative of one energy with respect to the position of the front, so the response of G to the front shape is a symmetric operator, and a term in the slope is antisymmetric. The condition fixes the ratio of the two slope couplings, b₃ = (1 − ν) b₂, whatever their source. With the loading of the task, G responds like tension with a slightly stronger restoring effect, 2/ρ = 2.17 in place of 2.

The equilibrium front. The mean of G = Γ fixes the load level. The varying part is one equation for the front shape, solved harmonic by harmonic: the front is a logarithm of the toughness profile's denominator. It lags by 0.203 ελ at the centre of a band and leads by 0.119 ελ midway between bands.

Along the front. The tension factor follows the toughness. The shear factors do not: the slope coupling adds a part that is odd about the band centre, so the shear along the front is largest slightly to one side of it.

All of this holds to first order in the amplitude of the front over its wavelength, for a bounded periodic front and a loading whose straight-front factors do not depend on the position of the front.

As in the paper, applied by us The first-order response is Eq. 15 of Gao and Rice (1986), read on the printed page; the same weights and couplings are in Lazarus (2011), Eqs. 15 to 17. Part B is our application of it. The two shear weights were recomputed from elasticity by one run's code (Section 8); the two slope couplings were not.

3 · What the solution says

The front lags in the tough bands, and shear makes the loading of the front lopsided

The front shape is even about each band: it lags most at the band centre and leads most midway between bands, 0.322 ελ from trough to crest. Under tension alone the same bands would give 0.350 ελ. The shear loading of the task stiffens the front by the factor 1/ρ = 1.086, because the shear modes together restore a perturbed front more strongly than tension does.

The odd part is the signature of the slope coupling. A solver without it reports shear factors that are even about the band; a solver with the wrong couplings also gets an odd part in the front itself, because the slope terms then no longer cancel in G. Both show up in the sixteen-point tables the task asks for.

Consequence of the solution A real-space solution of the equilibrium equation on a grid agrees with the closed-form front to 5 × 10⁻⁹, and G equals Γ to second order when the front is put back at finite ε.

4 · The test

The problem the agents received

Each agent received one file, the problem statement, in an otherwise empty folder. It defines the body, the stress intensity factors, Irwin's relation, the loading and the toughness pattern, and asks for the 76 numbers in a results file, with a method note and the code used, including the code of any checks. It does not name a paper, and it gives no formula for how the stress intensity factors depend on the front shape. That dependence is the task.

The full problem statement, as each run received it 
# A crack front trapped by tough bands under mixed-mode loading

Solve the problem below and report the requested numbers in `results.json`. The problem is fully stated here; the
method is your choice. Python 3 with NumPy and SciPy is available. Work only inside this folder.

## 1. The body and the crack

- An infinite, homogeneous, isotropic, linear elastic solid with Young's modulus E and Poisson's ratio **ν = 0.3**.
  Small strains, quasi-static loading, no body forces.
- Cartesian coordinates (x, y, z). A planar crack lies in the plane y = 0 and occupies the region x < a(z), where
  a(z) is a single-valued, smooth function. The crack front is the curve x = a(z), y = 0, which extends to
  z = ±∞. The crack faces are traction-free and do not touch.
- The crack can extend only within the plane y = 0 (it runs along a weak plane of the solid).

**Stress intensity factors.** At a point of the front, let **n** be the unit vector normal to the front, lying in
the plane y = 0 and pointing away from the crack (towards x > a), and let **t** = **n** × **e**<sub>y</sub>. For a
straight front x = const this gives **n** = **e**<sub>x</sub> and **t** = **e**<sub>z</sub>. With σ the stress
tensor evaluated in the plane y = 0 at distance r from the front along **n**, the stress intensity factors are

- K<sub>1</sub> = lim<sub>r→0</sub> √(2πr) σ<sub>yy</sub>,
- K<sub>2</sub> = lim<sub>r→0</sub> √(2πr) **e**<sub>y</sub>·σ·**n**,
- K<sub>3</sub> = lim<sub>r→0</sub> √(2πr) **e**<sub>y</sub>·σ·**t**.

**Energy release rate** per unit area of crack advance at a point of the front (Irwin's relation):

G = (1 − ν²)(K<sub>1</sub>² + K<sub>2</sub>²)/E + (1 + ν) K<sub>3</sub>²/E.

## 2. The loading

The crack is loaded from infinity only, and the loading does not change when the front moves. Its defining
property is this: for any straight front a(z) = const, wherever it lies, the stress intensity factors are uniform
along the front and equal to

K<sub>1</sub><sup>0</sup> > 0,   K<sub>2</sub><sup>0</sup> = 0.6 K<sub>1</sub><sup>0</sup>,   K<sub>3</sub><sup>0</sup> = 0.8 K<sub>1</sub><sup>0</sup>.

(Equivalently: every front considered here stays within a bounded distance of a straight line, and at distances
from the front, measured across it in a plane z = const, that are large compared with the wavelength and the
amplitude of its shape, the stresses tend to the leading, inverse-square-root stresses of a straight front with
these three factors.) G<sup>0</sup> denotes the energy release rate of a straight front.

## 3. Part A: a prescribed wavy front

Let the front be a(z) = ā + A cos(2πz/λ) with λ > 0 and A/λ ≪ 1 (ā is any constant). To first order in A/λ the
stress intensity factors and the energy release rate along this front can be written

K<sub>α</sub>(z) = K<sub>α</sub><sup>0</sup> + (A/λ) K<sub>1</sub><sup>0</sup> [C<sub>α</sub> cos(2πz/λ) + S<sub>α</sub> sin(2πz/λ)],   α = 1, 2, 3,

G(z) = G<sup>0</sup> + (A/λ) G<sup>0</sup> [C<sub>G</sub> cos(2πz/λ) + S<sub>G</sub> sin(2πz/λ)].

Report the eight dimensionless numbers C<sub>1</sub>, S<sub>1</sub>, C<sub>2</sub>, S<sub>2</sub>, C<sub>3</sub>, S<sub>3</sub>,
C<sub>G</sub>, S<sub>G</sub> for ν = 0.3 and the loading of Section 2.

## 4. Part B: the equilibrium front in tough bands

The fracture energy of the weak plane varies along z only:

Γ(z) = Γ<sub>0</sub> [1 + ε g(z/λ)],   g(s) = (1 − q²)/(1 − 2q cos 2πs + q²) − 1,   **q = 0.5**,

with 0 < ε ≪ 1. The function g has period 1 in s and zero mean; the tough bands are centred at s = 0, ±1, ±2, ….

The front is in equilibrium: **G(z) = Γ(z) at every point of the front**. The magnitude of the loading (at the
fixed ratios of Section 2) is the one for which such an equilibrium front exists.

Work to first order in ε. Write the front and the stress intensity factors as

a(z) = ā + ε λ f(z/λ),   with f bounded, periodic with period 1 (f(s + 1) = f(s)) and of zero mean over a period,

K<sub>α</sub>(z) = K<sub>α</sub><sup>0</sup> + ε K<sub>1</sub><sup>0</sup> κ<sub>α</sub>(z/λ),   α = 1, 2, 3,

where K<sub>α</sub><sup>0</sup> are the factors of the straight front under the same loading. Report:

1. `K10_scaled` = K<sub>1</sub><sup>0</sup> √((1 − ν²)/(E Γ<sub>0</sub>)) in the limit ε → 0;
2. `front_peak_to_peak` = max f − min f;
3. f(s), κ<sub>1</sub>(s), κ<sub>2</sub>(s) and κ<sub>3</sub>(s) at the sixteen points s = k/16, k = 0, 1, …, 15;
4. `kappa3_max`, the largest value of κ<sub>3</sub>(s) over a period, and `s_kappa3_max`, the point in
   −1/2 ≤ s < 1/2 where it occurs.

5 · GPT-6.1 Sol

The published response, recalled and tested where it could fail

The run wrote the complete first-order response, with both shear weights and both slope couplings as in the reference, and called it “the first-order in-plane crack-front weight-function relations in an infinite isotropic solid”. Its note gives no source and no derivation, and says that no web sources or external data were used. The relations are therefore recalled, not derived.

Two tests set its use of them apart. It fixed the signs of the slope terms from the local frame of the task and tested them at ν = 0, where they must reduce to the rotation of the two shear components. And it rebuilt the coefficients of G from the three stress intensity factors and required the sine term to vanish. Either test would have exposed the shear terms the other runs used.

Part B is in closed form, including the largest κ₃ from its stationarity condition instead of a search, and the sign and scale of the line operator are checked by quadrature in real space. Its note states what it did not do: no independent three-dimensional elastic computation.

6 · Kimi K3.1 Pro

The tension weight for every mode, and a result it states correctly when asked

Kimi wrote the response in Fourier space with the tension weight, −|k|/2, on all three modes, a slope coupling of 1 into the shear across the front and β = 2(1 − ν)/(2 − ν) into the shear along it. 7 · DeepSeek V4.1 Flash

Shear modes given the reverse of the tension response

DeepSeek took what its note calls the standard result, “mode I stable, shear modes unstable”: the nonlocal term of the two shear modes with the sign opposite to that of tension, and slope couplings of 1 from the projection of the shear traction onto the local frame. It attributes the response to Rice (1985). We have not checked that paper for these formulas; they are not those of Gao and Rice (1986).

The reversed sign changes the character of the answer. At the load ratios of the task the shear modes then outweigh tension, the net response of G to the front shape changes sign, and the front leads in the tough band by 0.56 ελ where the reference lags by 0.20 ελ.

The run named its weak point itself: the treatment of the shear modes is “the main unverified assumption of this solution”. Its numbers follow from its coefficients as exactly as Kimi's do. Of the five checks its note lists, two, a tension limit and two-dimensional limits of the operator, are not carried out by any file in its folder, nor by the five working scripts it deleted before finishing (Section 10).

Asked afterwards for the formulas of Gao and Rice (1986), the model gave a third set, different from both the paper and its own run, and marked several of its coefficients “sure”.

8 · GLM-5.3

The response computed from elasticity: the weights right, the couplings exchanged

GLM did not write the response down. It computed it: the operator that relates slip to traction on the crack plane in Fourier space, an exact factorisation for tension, and for the two coupled shear modes a least-squares solution of the finite-part integral equation for one Fourier mode of the front. The run produced 27 files in 149 minutes.

Its shear weights are the published ones. In its notation they are −1/2 + γ and −1/2 − γ with γ = ν/(2 − ν), which are (2 − 3ν)/(2 − ν) and (2 + ν)/(2 − ν) halved. We reran its scripts in a container without network: at ν = 0.2, 0.25, 0.3 and 0.35 they return these weights to about 10⁻⁵, by both of its ways of reading the stress intensity factors from its solution. We have not checked its code line by line.

Its coupling between the shear modes has the published magnitude, γ, and the opposite sign in the fixed frame. In the local frame of the task this exchanges the two slope couplings: 14/17 into the shear across the front and 20/17 into the shear along it, where the reference has 20/17 and 14/17. With them G has a sine term, −2.27, and five of the eight Part A numbers pass: the ones that depend on the weights alone.

The run had the evidence in hand. Its two readings of K, from the traction ahead of the front and from the slip behind it, agree on the weights and disagree on exactly this coupling. Points follow the physics. DeepSeek did not have the result and chose among guesses. 13 · Lessons

What this case study teaches, and what it does not

Each lesson rests on four runs of one problem: observations to test on new problems, not measured rates.

Domain knowledge

Tension is not a template for shear

Two runs carried the tension response over to the shear modes, with the same weight or with its sign reversed. The published weights differ for each mode and depend on Poisson's ratio, and the shear modes are coupled through the slope, which tension is not.

Kimi and DeepSeek method notes (Sections 6 and 7).

Domain knowledge

An energy has a symmetric response

G is the derivative of one energy with respect to the front position, so it cannot depend on the slope of the front at first order. All three failing answers have such a term, −0.47, −1.14 and −2.27 as the sine coefficient of G. One run saw it and explained it; the run that passed tested for it.

Part A values of each run (Appendix A).

Verification

Residuals of your own equations do not test them

Kimi's eight checks pass near 10⁻¹⁵ and its answer scores 12 of 76. Each check re-evaluates the equations it started from. A check can only catch an error it does not share.

Kimi's solve_checks.txt and verify.py.

Verification

Two routes that disagree are the finding

GLM read the stress intensity factors from its solution in two ways. They disagreed on the coupling of the shear modes, the one quantity it got wrong. It resolved the disagreement by an argument that is not in its files, and reported the route it kept.

GLM method note, check 6; its check_sliproute.py, rerun by us.

Recall

Within reach is not the same as retrieved

Kimi states the published formulas correctly when the paper is named, and did not use them when only the problem was posed. A model's answer to a direct question does not tell what it will bring to a problem.

The question asked afterwards and its answer (Section 6).

Running the study

A reader that cannot compute should not judge arithmetic

The second model's answers on the source of the theory and the place of the first error were the same in every grading of a run. On one claim that needed a division at sixteen points it was wrong in five of six answers. Checks that need numbers should be rerun, not read.

Grading records; results record, section on the grader.

What the study does not show: that one model is better than another, that the run that passed would pass again, that any of the three would fail again, or that the result would be the same with web access. Each would need repeated runs under controlled conditions.

14 · Sources and scope

Study basis

One problem, four runs, one per model, through two agent programs: a case study, not a ranking. The reference comes from a published result that we read and applied; the readings of the runs come from an AI model and from us. No expert in fracture mechanics has reviewed the benchmark or this note, and the authors of the paper have not been contacted.

The agents' words quoted here come from files they saved, their method notes. Their private reasoning was not used. The question put to two models after their runs is not part of the benchmark and did not affect any score.

Where this case departs from our usual practice: the grading by a second model was set up and tested on six control submissions on the day of the runs; the run of GPT-6.1 Sol was added after the first two results were known, under the same conditions; the points rubric was written after the runs; and the problem statement was reviewed by two AI models, not yet read by an outside expert.
