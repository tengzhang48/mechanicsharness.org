Dimensional analysis of the crack-tip condition (log P385–P387)

Teng: “why the undrained picture is not near tip” (P385); “We need some dimensionless analysis, like the Pi theorem?” (P386); “please document this” (P387).

In one line: the Pi theorem applied to the field, not only to the global answer, shows the condition the agents missed. “Small time” has no meaning until you say small compared with which length, and at the crack tip that length is the distance r itself.

1. The physics: why the undrained picture fails at the tip

“Undrained” means that the fluid has not had time to move. That holds where the pore pressure varies over a length ℓ with t ≪ ℓ²/c. - In the bulk, p varies over the crack size L. The solid is undrained for t ≪ L²/c. - At the tip, the undrained solution is singular. - p follows the mean stress, so p ~ K/√r and ∇p ~ r^(−3/2). By Darcy’s law the flux at the tip is infinite at t = 0⁺. - The pressure varies over the distance r itself, so the local drainage time is ~r²/c, which tends to 0 as r → 0. - At any t > 0 the zone r ≲ √(ct) has drained. - Inside that zone: - the solid carries the load through its skeleton with the drained ν; - p vanishes at the tip: Hui, Long & Ning 2013, “The pore pressure at the crack tip vanishes immediately after loading”, with p ~ √r there (their Eq. 20). - Outside the zone the solid is still undrained, with ν_u. - Analogy: small-scale yielding, or the creep zone of a suddenly loaded crack (Riedel & Rice, cited by Hui et al. 2013). The tip relaxes first because the fields there are strongest and change fastest. - Our FE (FE_CHECK.md): the near-tip K follows the drained relation in a plateau at r/√(ct) ≈ 0.03–0.15. It returns to the undrained relation at r/√(ct) ≈ 10–30.

2. Pi analysis

2.1 Global (what the agents did). K/(σ√(πL)) = f(ct/L², ν, ν_u, α). - The agents took ct/L² ≪ 1 and concluded “undrained everywhere”. - This group compares time with L only, so it cannot see the tip.

2.2 The field: a second group. σ_yy(r, θ, t) near the tip depends on two lengths, r and √(ct), so ρ = r/√(ct) appears. The two orders of limits are the two ends of ρ:

Limit ρ State at the point Intensity obtained
t → 0 at fixed r → ∞ undrained the outer K = 1 (units σ√(πL))
r → 0 at fixed t > 0 (the definition of K) → 0 drained K_tip

2.3 The inner problem is self-similar. - Near the tip the only lengths are r and √(ct), and the load is K_outer. - So K_tip/K_outer = Φ(ν, ν_u, α), with no time dependence: no second length exists to form a group with √(ct). Hui et al. 2013 find the same similarity variable r/√(D_c t) (their Eq. 9d). - Consequence: K(0⁺) = Φ·K_outer, and the ratio survives t → 0⁺. K(0⁺) = 1 holds only if Φ = 1.

2.4 Rescaling the equations reduces Φ to one group. - Normalizing p removes α. The inner problem depends only on η = (ν_u − ν)/(1 − ν_u), so K_tip/K_outer = Φ(η) with Φ(0) = 1. This is Grok’s review of B (“independent of α”), with the weak-coupling expansion Φ = 1 − (3/4)η + O(η²). - Two different η’s. η here is not Detournay & Cheng’s η = α(1 − 2ν)/[2(1 − ν)]. - For a gel (ν_u = ½), η = 1 − 2ν and 1 + η = 2(1 − ν). So 1/(1 + η) is the base 1/[2(1 − ν)] of Yu et al.’s Eq. 12.

2.5 What the Pi theorem cannot give: the value of Φ. That takes the solution (a Wiener–Hopf factorization).

3. Evidence from the runs on B

  • No non-GPT run formed r/√(ct) or any similarity variable (0 of 9; grep of the ANSWER.md files).
  • All nine used the global group ct/L² ≪ 1, and judged the √(ct) tip zone by its share of the crack volume (WHY_DRAINED_TIP_MISSED.md).
  • GPT formed the tip variable:
    • MP-39: X = (x − L)/√(c/s), the Laplace-domain form of r/√(ct);
    • MP-37 and MP-38: compared wavelength with √(c/s) through m = √(k² + s/c).

5. A caution, and a draft knowledge entry (not yet approved)

The caution (P283, ../../KNOWLEDGE_ENTRY_DESIGN.md). A generic “check the boundary first” instruction produced checklists, but did not make agents find the decisive boundary more often. A generic “do dimensional analysis” instruction may fail the same way. So the entry below follows the P283 format: it names the decisive comparison and the tempting wrong one.

Draft entry: fields near a singular point under a sudden load (diffusion-coupled solids). - Decisive comparison: the distance r to the singular point against the diffusion length √(ct). - The tempting wrong comparison is the body size L against √(ct). - The assumption it invalidates: “undrained (instantaneous) response everywhere at t = 0⁺”. It fails for r ≲ √(ct) at every t > 0. - What replaces it: an inner drained zone, self-similar in r/√(ct), matched to the outer undrained field. - Pointwise quantities at the singular point (K, J*, the tip pressure) come from the inner zone: K_tip = Φ(η)·K_outer, with η = (ν_u − ν)/(1 − ν_u). - Φ needs a solution, e.g. a Wiener–Hopf factorization. Check point: Φ(1/3) = 0.807593 with drained faces. - What remains usable from the outer solution: bulk quantities (volumes, far fields, face pressures away from the tip) and K_outer as the load on the inner problem. - Independent checks: - Φ(0) = 1; - the weak-coupling slope dΦ/dη = −3/4; - the energy identity n₁₁² + n₂₁² = 1 + η; - with impermeable faces, Φ = (1 + η)^(−1/2). - Test (proposed): G1 and G2 in the next round, with and without this entry, for the agents that missed the drained tip on B.
