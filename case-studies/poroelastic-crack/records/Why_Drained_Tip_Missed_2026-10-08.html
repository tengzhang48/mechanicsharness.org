Why the drained tip was missed (log P381)

Teng: “why the drained tip was missed? That is something we can look into details. If you were working on it, will you be able to find it”.

Evidence. The nine non-GPT runs on problem B were read: - K3: MP-22, 24, 33; - GLM: MP-06, 10, 21; - DeepSeek: MP-13, 27, 30.

We read their ANSWER.md files and grepped them (tip zone, singularity, order of limits). For the three GPT runs, we searched the session logs for the first mention of the tip structure. Quotes are pasted from those files.

The decisive step

K is defined at the tip: K(t) = lim_{r→0} √(2πr) σ_yy at fixed t > 0. - The undrained t = 0⁺ field is singular there. The pore pressure goes as r^(−1/2), so its gradient goes as r^(−3/2). - The tip drains at once. At distance r the local drainage time is ~r²/c, which tends to 0 as r → 0. So at every t > 0 a region of size ~√(ct) around the tip has drained. K, being a limit at r → 0, is set inside that region. - The two limits do not commute. Taking t → 0 first, at fixed r, gives the outer undrained value 1. Taking r → 0 first gives 0.8076.

What the nine runs did

1. The same chain in all nine. - “Instantaneous response = undrained elasticity” (MP-22). - K of a traction problem does not depend on the elastic constants, so K(0⁺) = K(∞) = 1. - Two quotes: “SIF of the Griffith crack depends only on face tractions” (MP-21); “the undrained/drained distinction does not [change K]” (MP-30). - Variants of the same answer: MP-13 reports √π, the same value in other units; MP-27 reports 1/(1 − ν), a different error. - No run mentions the order of limits (0 of 9).

2. The tip zone was noticed, then dismissed by a scaling that dropped the singularity. - Five runs name the √(ct) zone around the tip: - “The crack-tip regions (size ~√(ct)) contribute only O((ct){3/4}L{1/2}) to V, which is higher order” (MP-22); - “Tip zones of size sqrt(ct) contribute only O((ct)^{3/4})” (MP-24); - MP-10 and MP-33 say the same; MP-13 has “A boundary layer of width √(ct) around each tip”. - They judged the zone by its share of the crack volume, never by its effect on K. - The (ct)^{3/4} estimate holds only for bounded fields inside the zone. With the r^(−1/2) field the tip zones contribute at order √t, the same order as the faces. Grok: “Tip contribution scaling as t^{3/4} rather than √t (would happen if Z were bounded rather than r^{-1/2})”. - One run had the missing piece and did not connect it. K3 MP-24 wrote that the undrained pressure “inherits the 1/sqrt(r) elastic singularity” at the tips, but did not draw the consequence (infinite gradient, so instant local drainage).

3. One run computed the right physics and explained it away (GLM MP-06, check 4). - Its own transient simulation gave: - “fit over ct in [4e-4,4e-3]: A_fit = 2.69359 (analytic 1.12838, diff 138.71%)”; - K “≈ 0.8–0.84 instead of 1”. - It attributed both to numerics: “unresolved tip singularities of the Skempton initial field in the FFT trace (they also pollute the pipeline’s K, which came out ≈ 0.8–0.84 instead of 1)”. - It reported the analytic values, 1 and 1.128. - The simulation’s values were near the truth (K = 0.8076, A = 2.4402), but partly by a numerical route. Correction P382, from the artifacts partC.py and partC_out.txt: - The pipeline adds a drained crack under unit traction (K = 1) to the crack problem of the pore-pressure eigenstress, computed by FFT on an 800 × 500 grid. - The second part prints “K/sqrt(pi L) = -0.18009” at t = 0⁺, so the total is 0.82 at t = 0⁺, and 0.814–0.841 at later outputs. - The value 0.82 appears before any diffusion step. It comes from the grid smoothing the singular undrained pressure at the tip. That acts like a drained tip zone of grid size; it is not resolved drainage. The agent’s attribution to “unresolved tip singularities” was partly right. - The failure was not testing it. There was no grid refinement, and no question about what physics a smoothed tip would imply. A refinement would have shown whether the 0.82 depends on the grid; by self-similarity it should not. - The analysis (K = 1) was wrong. - The same pattern appears in print. Bouklas, Landis & Huang 2015 attributed the immersed short-time drop in J* to “the first time step” (../gel/LITERATURE_CHECK.md).

What GPT did differently

GPT read the definition of K literally and asked what the field is at the tip for t > 0. - MP-37 asked this at 13 min into the session: “the bulk undrained W gives K_outer = 1 but true near-tip K may differ”. - MP-38 proposed the energy-matching value 0.866 at 39 min (“rice/cleary small-scale drainage energy matching … k1_0=sqrt((1-nu_u)/(1-nu))=0.866025!”), then moved past it to the factorization. - MP-39: “At every fixed positive time, arbitrarily close to the tip the mechanical singularity is drained. The outer initial field is undrained. Their intensities need not agree.”

Lessons (candidates for knowledge entries; “name the decisive comparison”)

The dimensional analysis behind lessons 2 and 3, with Φ(η) and a draft entry, is in DIMENSIONAL_ANALYSIS.md (P387).

  1. A pointwise quantity is set by the innermost zone, however small that zone is. K is not weighted by area.
  2. A scaling estimate must carry the singularity. Bounded fields gave (ct)^{3/4}; the r^(−1/2) field gives √t.
  3. “Undrained at t = 0⁺” holds in the bulk, not at a singular point. Compare the local drainage time r²/c with t; at a crack tip it tends to 0. Take r → 0 first for K.
  4. When your own numerics disagree with your analysis, examine the analysis where they disagree before blaming the numerics (MP-06; Bouklas et al. 2015).
