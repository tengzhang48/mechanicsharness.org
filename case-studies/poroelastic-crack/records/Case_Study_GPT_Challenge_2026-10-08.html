GPT Challenge Case Study

Case study: an AI agent challenged a published heuristic, and the challenge held (log P361–P368)

Teng: “This is very interesting.” … “challenging is the first step to get new insight.” … “please document, this is interesting.”

The problem

Problem B of the multiphysics round, written open book from Detournay & Cheng 1991 (“Plane strain analysis of a stationary hydraulic fracture in a poroelastic medium”, Int. J. Solids Struct. 27(13), 1645–1662). - Setup: a crack of length 2L in an infinite Biot poroelastic solid in plane strain, suddenly loaded. - Loading 1: a normal-stress step on the faces, which are drained (p = 0). - Asked: the stress intensity factor just after loading, K(0⁺), and the small-time growth of the crack volume, V(t) = V(0⁺) + A√(ct/L²).

The paper’s answers, used as the key: - K(0⁺) = [(1 − ν_u)/(1 − ν)] √(πL) (Eq. 29). That is 0.75√(πL) for ν = 0.2, ν_u = 0.4. The paper calls it a “heuristic argument” (p. 1650): the instantaneous undrained opening, read with the drained near-tip relation, because “the crack tips are effectively drained”. - A = 16(1 − ν_u)(ν_u − ν)/[√π(1 − ν)] = 1.354 (Eq. 18). It comes from one-dimensional diffusion through the faces, combined with a reciprocal-theorem argument.

GPT, in all three runs and naming no source: - It named the structure: “The inner elastic field is drained while the outer opening is undrained.” - It made the definition precise: “K1_0 is ambiguous without an order of limits”. lim_{t→0} lim_{r→0} gives the true tip value; the reverse order gives exactly 1. - It solved the tip problem by a Wiener–Hopf-type factorization and checked it with an energy identity that includes the pressure-gradient term. - It concluded that “a purely elastic modulus-ratio estimate does not suffice”. That modulus-ratio estimate is exactly the paper’s heuristic. - It found three real gaps in our problem statement: shear traction not stated; the order of limits for K(0⁺); the literal face pressure is 0.

How the challenge was checked

1. Cross-family review: Codex (grading/review_B_K10-codex/output/VERDICT.md). - Method: an independent derivation from the problem statement (a Laplace–Fourier reduction, then a Wiener–Hopf factorization solved by more than one method), plus a finite-crack check. - K(0⁺) = 0.8075930√(πL). “The drained inner tip opening has an amplitude 1.07679067 times its matched undrained outer amplitude. The paper’s Eq. (29) effectively sets this matching factor to one.” “The paper’s 0.75 is neither of these two stress limits.” - A = 2.4402 = 1.3541 from the flat faces (the paper’s term) + 1.0862 from the two tips, which “survives at order √τ, even though the tip region shrinks”. - Grok’s review (review_B_K10/grok/VERDICT.md, P372): K(0⁺) = 0.80759 ± 0.00005 and A = 2.4402 ± 0.001, supporting GPT. Its factorization was rewritten from scratch, with the same kernel. A finite-crack spectral check extrapolates to 0.8074–0.8075. - On the paper’s mesh: “Fig. 9’s approach to 0.75 is therefore not an independent confirmation of a resolved inner tip”. - On the weak-coupling expansion: “K = 1 − (3/4)η + O(η²)”, which at η = 1/3 coincides with 0.75 at first order.

2. Opus’s direct simulation (FE_CHECK.md; FEniCSx, 2D plane strain, Biot poroelasticity, u P2 and p P1, backward Euler; meshes of 5–40 thousand cells graded to 10⁻⁵–10⁻⁶ L at the tip). - K(0⁺)/√(πL) ≈ 0.80 ± 0.01: - stable across meshes and time steps; - the drained plateau visible at r ≲ 0.15√(ct), with the undrained field outside; - insensitive to resolving the face layer. - A ≈ 2.2–2.4 with the face layer resolved: - the local slope is 2.21 at t = 1.1×10⁻⁵, rising as t → 0; - a tip-only mesh gave 1.07–1.09, which matches Codex’s tip part; - two of Opus’s meshing attempts failed first and are recorded.

What it shows

  1. For this problem, the published heuristic is about 7% low, and the published small-time volume misses a tip contribution of the same order.
    • The paper itself called Eq. 29 heuristic, and Teng suspected an approximation (“I have a feeling 0.75 has some approxmination”).
    • A plausible reason the paper’s numerical curve (Fig. 9) seemed to support it: six quadratic elements over 2L (p. 1655) cannot resolve a tip layer of width √(ct) ≈ 0.01L at t* = 10⁻⁴. This is Opus’s reading, not verified.
    • We have not checked whether later literature already corrected Eq. 29 or Eq. 18.
  2. An AI agent proposed the correction itself. It did so unprompted, consistently over three runs, with an argument and a checkable value, and named no source. Two independent checks confirmed it. This is the first case in the pilot of an agent producing a checked correction to a published result. The correction is already in the literature (see “Literature check”): GPT re-derived a known exact result, it did not discover a new one.
  3. Challenge, then verification, is the right process.
    • The automatic checks marked GPT “wrong” against the key; without a check, the challenge would have been graded as an error.
    • The grading now records challenges and grades them both ways (P363), and the index has a challenge component (MECHANICS_INDEX_CONCEPT.md).
  4. Agents differ in exactly this capability. Only GPT found the drained tip; DeepSeek and GLM kept the elastic intuition (“SIF fixed by crack-face tractions”). This is the “knowing the boundary” lesson, now with a model that knew it.
  5. Keys must be derived and challengeable. This is the third source with a problem in its equations, after Detournay 2016 Eq. 16 and Suo et al. Eq. 7.3. Here the problem is a heuristic, not a misprint.

Literature check (P369): the correction is already known

Yu, Bouklas, Landis & Huang 2020 (“Poroelastic effects on the time- and rate-dependent fracture of polymer gels”, J. Appl. Mech. 87, 031005; uploaded by Teng) give the short-time limit for a stationary crack in a linearly poroelastic gel, citing Atkinson & Craster [21] and Yu et al. [24] (p. 031005-4). - The result: K_I(t → 0⁺) = K_I0 [1/(2(1 − ν))]^α (Eq. 12). - α ≈ 0.735 for the immersed case, with the crack faces in contact with solvent, which drains them as in problem B’s loading 1; - α = 0.5 for the not-immersed case; - here ν_u = 0.5 (incompressible constituents), so 1/(2(1 − ν)) = (1 − ν_u)/(1 − ν). - Detournay & Cheng’s Eq. 29 is this form with α = 1. The exact short-time analysis replaces the exponent 1 by about 0.735 for drained faces. - For problem B’s values (ν = 0.2, ν_u = 0.4), the value found by GPT, Codex and the FE, 0.8076 = 0.75^α, corresponds to α ≈ 0.743 (ours). That is the same structure, with α depending weakly on the parameters. The sources Yu et al. 2020 cite for it, Atkinson & Craster [21] and Yu et al. [24], are not on disk. The 2020 result itself is reproduced below (P371).

What this changes: - GPT’s challenge is correct, and it agrees with the established exact result. GPT did not discover a new correction: it re-derived a known one, without naming the source (it named none). - Point 2 of “What it shows” is restated accordingly. It is the first case in the pilot of an agent producing a checked correction to a source paper’s result; the correction exists in later or parallel literature (Atkinson & Craster; Yu et al.). - The lesson for keys stands, and is sharper: a key taken from one paper can be superseded by other literature. “Open book” should include checking whether the result was later corrected.

Did GPT know the literature result? (Teng, P370: “So, we are unsure whether GPT knows the new results”) We cannot be sure. The evidence separates the number from the method. 1. The number is computed, not quoted. The published gel exponent α ≈ 0.735 (ν_u = 0.5) would give 0.75^0.735 = 0.809. GPT’s 0.8076 corresponds to α = 0.743 for ν = 0.2, ν_u = 0.4, which no paper on disk prints. Its factor_check.py computes a numerical Wiener–Hopf factorization, and the value agrees with Codex’s independent computation to 7 digits. 2. Literature-informed reasoning, no citation of the result. - MP-38’s log considers “Rice/Cleary small-scale drainage energy matching G_tip=G_outer” (the 0.866 bound) and moves past it to compute the factor. - “Atkinson”, “Craster”, “Bouklas”, “Landis”, “Huang” and “Hui” appear in no GPT log or file. 3. Prior knowledge of the structure (a drained tip; the modulus-ratio guess corrected) cannot be excluded.

Test: variants whose answer no paper prints (other parameters or boundary conditions), so that recall can supply at most the method. Proposed for the gel problems (G1).

Literature check, continued (P376): Hui et al. 2013 and Bouklas et al. 2015

Teng uploaded both papers. Details are in ../gel/LITERATURE_CHECK.md. - Hui, Long & Ning 2013 (J. Appl. Mech. 80, 021014) solved the same short-time tip problem for a gel. - From an approximation (σ₁₁ = 0 on the crack faces) they found K_tip = K_I/[2(1 − ν)] (Eq. 24), “one of our key results”, and reported FE agreement. - This is α = 1, the same as Detournay & Cheng’s heuristic. - The literature therefore holds three statements of this one quantity: - α = 1: Detournay & Cheng 1991 (heuristic) and Hui et al. 2013 (approximation and FE); - α ≈ 0.735: Atkinson & Craster 1991 and Yu et al. 2018, cited by Yu et al. 2020. - GPT’s challenge sides with the second. So do Codex, Grok, and our FE, which excludes α = 1 at ν = 0.24 (0.736–0.741 against 0.658). - Bouklas, Landis & Huang 2015 (J. Appl. Mech. 82, 081007; nonlinear FE). For the immersed specimen they found the instantaneous J* “poor” against LEFM, and attributed it to the first time step (“solvent diffusion occurs almost instantaneously (over the first time step) … leading to a lower J*”). - Our reading: this is at least partly the physical drained tip layer, which gives J*(0⁺) = 0.82J₀ (Yu et al. 2020; our FE).

Conclusion. Yu et al.’s short-time limit is reproduced to about 0.2% by GPT’s factorization and to about 1% by an independent FE. GPT’s B answer is the same exact tip solution at a different ν_u. Not reproduced: Yu’s finite-element time histories (Fig. 3) and the delayed-fracture window. Those would be the subject of the gel problems.

Caveats

  • Grok’s verdict supports GPT (P372).
  • The FE value of A is extrapolated (about ±0.1).
  • All three checks share the stated model (plane strain, drained shear-free faces, Biot). That model is the problem as posed.
