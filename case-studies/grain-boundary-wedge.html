Fracture mechanics case study · AI agents and grain-boundary diffusion · 8 October 2026 round, written up 25 October

Atoms diffusing into a grain boundary: can AI agents find how fast a film relaxes, and the crack-like wedge it leaves?

Read the mechanics: model and solutionSee how the agents did

Gao, Zhang, Nix, Thompson and Arzt (1999) showed that atoms diffusing from the free surface of a tensile film into a grain boundary pinned at the substrate insert a wedge that relaxes the boundary’s traction exponentially and turns the boundary, elastically, into an edge crack whose tip sits at the interface. We gave their single-wedge problem, without the paper, to AI agents. All four Kimi and GLM answers matched the paper’s decay rates to one part in 10⁵; the five DeepSeek answers ranged from 0.60 to 188 against 11.74.

Aim

To see whether agents carry a singular integro-differential eigenproblem through to converged numbers, check them with something that could fail, and tell a computed limit from a remembered one.

Our question
Given the elasticity of the wedge, the diffusion law and the boundary conditions at the surface and the interface, can an AI agent find the rates at which the boundary traction decays, the shape of the slowest mode, what the boundary becomes at long times and its stress intensity factor, and the time scale of relaxation?
Posed to twelve runs on 8 October 2026 with a 170-minute budget. The paper and the key were withheld.
The answer and its present limit

Kimi MP-02 and MP-09 and GLM MP-03 and MP-05 solved the singular eigenproblem and matched the paper’s λ₁ = 11.74265 and λ₂ = 264.6348 to one part in 10⁵; each also computed the edge-crack limit, 1.12152. The ungraded DeepSeek rerun MP-47 matched to one part in 10⁴. Only Kimi MP-09 was graded right on every part. What the agents did · what separated them · why it matters

What the agents did

Every answer reduced the problem to an eigenproblem for traction modes, λf = −∫S f‴ with the three boundary conditions, and named the long-time state a traction-free edge crack; MP-20’s written equations had sign slips. They differed in how they discretized the singular operator.

What separated them

Whether the discrete operator kept all three boundary conditions and the right signs and factors, and whether a check compared it with something outside itself: a second discretization, or the edge-crack value computed from the same kernel.

Why it matters

The wrong runs reported checks that passed: a residual of 10⁻⁷, five stable digits, λ₂ > λ₁. Those checks shared the defect, and MP-20 delivered its number past two time checks that had failed. The round’s headline count had a gap too: λ₁ was not in it.

1 · The model

Atoms flow into a tensile grain boundary that is pinned at the substrate

A polycrystalline film of thickness h lies on a substrate with the same isotropic elastic constants, so the two form an elastic half-space in plane strain. A grain boundary runs from the free surface, z = 0, to the film/substrate interface, z = h. It starts under a uniform normal tension σ₀.

An atom on the boundary has chemical potential −σgbΩ, lower than on the flat free surface when the boundary is in tension. Atoms therefore diffuse from the surface into the boundary and plate onto it, inserting a wedge of opening 2u(z, t). The wedge is a continuous array of climb-edge dislocations, and its elastic field, built from the solution for an edge dislocation near a free surface, lowers the traction on the boundary.

At the surface the traction is zero, because the chemical potential must match the flat surface; surface diffusion is taken as fast and grooving is neglected. At the interface the film neither slides nor exchanges atoms: no flux, ∂σgb/∂z = 0, and no opening rate, ∂²σgb/∂z² = 0.

2 · The solution

Each mode decays at its own rate; the slowest sets the time, and a crack is what remains

One equation for the traction. Mass conservation makes the opening rate proportional to −∂²σgb/∂z², so the rate of adding dislocations is set by the third derivative of the traction. The kernel turns that into a traction rate. The result is a linear integro-differential equation for σgb alone, and separating variables gives modes fn that decay as e−λnt/t₀.

A crack tip at the interface. Integrating f‴ three times with the three conditions writes f as an integral of f‴ against a known kernel G(x, ξ), and turns the eigenproblem into a Cauchy-type singular integral equation for the dislocation density f‴. That density diverges as (1 − x)−1/2 at the interface: the wedge closes like a crack. The paper solves it by Gauss–Chebyshev collocation with this singularity built in, and two methods agree: λ₁ = 11.74265 and λ₂ = 264.6348 (Table 1).

What remains at long times. Every mode decays, so σgb → 0 along the whole boundary while the plated wedge keeps it open. A cut from the surface to depth h with traction-free faces, in a half-space under σ₀, is an edge crack, and the stress intensity factor at the interface tends to 1.1215 σ₀√(πh). The paper shows K approaching “the value associated with a traction-free edge crack” on a time scale t₀/λ₁ (Fig. 7) but does not print the number. 1.1215 is the handbook edge-crack value, and the model’s own traction-free equation gives it too: our collocation gives 1.1175, 1.1195 and 1.1205 at N = 30, 60 and 120, and the benchmark audit, carried to N = 480 and extrapolated, gives 1.121522.

A closed-form estimate. If the first mode’s wedge is given the opening profile of a crack, f₁″ = −3√(1 − x²), the decay rate follows as λ₁ ≈ 3π/∫f₁ dx = 13.36, 13.8% above the exact value. The key also notes that the paper’s text rounds the decay ratios to 25 for the second mode, nearly 400 for the fourth and nearly 1000 for the fifth, while Table 1 gives 22.5, 115.6, 327 and 709.

3 · What the solution says

The film relaxes from the surface down, and leaves a crack-tip field at the interface

The slowest mode is zero at the free surface and largest at the interface: the boundary relaxes first where atoms arrive and last where the film is held. At mid-depth it is 0.802 of its interface value. Its wedge opening, −f₁″, stays wide over the upper part of the film and closes as √(1 − x) at the interface, close to the opening of a crack. That resemblance is the basis of the paper’s crack-shape estimate, which lands within 14% of the exact rate.

At first the deeper traction moves the other way. The material plated near the surface pushes the grains apart there and loads the part of the boundary below it, so the deeper traction rises before it decays: at the interface it is 1.010 σ₀ at t = 0.001 t₀ and 0.85 σ₀ at 0.05 t₀. The grader of MP-05 derived the initial rate at depth x, 16/x³ in units of σ₀/t₀.

The higher modes die quickly: the second decays 22.5 times faster than the first, and the paper finds the process “for the most part dominated by the first eigenmode”. The paper puts complete relaxation on a time scale “of order t₀/λ₁”. The value 0.085 t₀ is the e-folding time of the first mode, not an end point, and the problem names no measure: at the interface the traction halves by 0.095 t₀ and falls to 5% by 0.291 t₀. Because t₀ grows as h³, halving the film thickness makes every one of these times eight times shorter.

The paper’s discussion draws the consequence: the wedge creates a crack-like stress concentration where the boundary meets the substrate without creating any free surface, and that concentration may nucleate dislocations in the film.

Consequence of the solution Not requested: the late-time law σgb ≈ c₁σ₀f₁e−λ₁t/t₀ has c₁ ≈ 1.53 for f₁(1) = 1, by the graders of MP-02, MP-05 and MP-09 (1.531); the discrete projection of the uniform initial traction converges as 1/N, from 1.5289 at N = 120 (our check) to 1.5305 at N = 480, and extrapolates to 1.531048 (benchmark audit).

4 · The test

The problem the agents received

The problem states the geometry, the traction integral with its kernel, the diffusion law, the boundary and initial conditions and the time unit t₀. It asks parts (a)–(c) and a results file. It does not name the paper, give any eigenvalue or the edge-crack factor, or say what “relaxation time” means.

The round ran on 8 October. This was its long problem, with a 170-minute budget and a 3-hour timeout; the others had 50 minutes. Each answer was rerun from its reproduce command, checked automatically and graded blind by Codex on the three parts, with every error classified. The first three DeepSeek runs had reasoning switched off by a configuration error; three reasoning-on reruns followed. GPT was run on the poroelastic problem only. MP-47’s blind grade was deferred to save Codex usage and has not been run.

What was asked, abridged

(a) How σgb(z, t) evolves; the late-time decay rate λ₁ and the next rate λ₂.

(b) What the grain boundary becomes as t → ∞, and the stress intensity factor at z = h in units of σ₀√(πh).

(c) The time scale of relaxation, in units of t₀.

Report: λ₁, λ₂, the slowest mode at z/h = 0.5 with f(1) = 1, K∞, the relaxation time and the method.

9 · What separated them

Where the runs diverged

Eight of nine answers wrote the eigenproblem correctly, and every answer named the edge crack. The runs diverged in the discrete operator: whether it kept all three boundary conditions with the right signs and factors and was converged. They diverged again in their checks: the exact runs compared their result with something outside the operator, while the checks the wrong runs reported could not see their defects, and MP-20 set aside two time checks that had failed.

11 · Lessons

What this case study teaches, and what it does not

Each lesson rests on a few runs of one problem: observations to test on new problems, not measured rates.

Domain knowledge

The wedge tip is a crack tip; put it in the basis

The density diverges as (1 − x)−1/2 at the interface. The four exact Kimi and GLM runs built that into their basis. MP-47 used up to 400 points of a plain Chebyshev basis for four digits, and MP-40 stalled about 1% off.

Method strings in results.json; MP-47 answer, lines 83–88; MP-40 answer, lines 62–79.

Verification

A check that shares the defect cannot find it

MP-44’s residual and convergence used the same inconsistent basis; MP-20’s own-number checks passed with λ₁ twenty times too small. Compare with something the operator does not control.

MP-44 grade, section 5; MP-20 grade, section 5.

Verification

A computed limit is a check; a remembered one is not

Computing K = 1.12152 from the kernel tests its sign and normalization. Four runs typed in 1.1215, and the K check passed them all, including two with λ₁ wrong.

MP-18, MP-40 and MP-44 grades; MP-47 solve.py, line 25.

Proof organization

A late-time law needs its amplitude

MP-02 left out the modal amplitude and MP-05 normalized it at the wrong point. Rates and shape were right; the stated late-time traction was 20 to 35% too low.

Define the relaxation time

MP-03 gave both the e-folding time, 0.085 t₀, and the time for 95% relaxation at mid-depth, 0.27 t₀, because the problem did not say which. At the interface the 95% time is 0.291 t₀, outside the key’s band. The round notes list this as an ambiguity to fix.

12 · Sources and scope

Study basis

One problem, twelve runs and nine answers, two or three per setting: a case study, not a ranking. The key and the grades come from AI models. The key was checked against the paper’s Table 1 and Appendix B, by our rerun of its collocation and by a separate audit of the benchmark; the grades were not regraded. No expert has reviewed them.

The agents’ words quoted here come from files they saved: ANSWER.md of MP-02, MP-03, MP-05, MP-09, MP-18 and MP-20, and MP-44’s solve.py. Private reasoning was not used.

Every file this page links is in the case package: the task, the key and checker, every saved answer, the grades, and the records cited here. Session transcripts are not included.

Execution records

DeepSeek with reasoning off. The first three DeepSeek runs went through Cline without its thinking flag, a configuration error. They are kept with that label; three reasoning-on reruns were added.

Harness stops. Cline ended MP-07 twice and MP-20 once on a compaction-summary turn at about 6.5 and 19 minutes; MP-20’s fresh rerun is the answer here, and MP-07 has none. How the runs ended

Nine runs delivered an answer within the 170-minute budget, between 28 and 107 minutes; MP-07, MP-15 and MP-32 did not.
